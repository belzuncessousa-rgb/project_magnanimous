## Lore

### Identity & Character

Mognagna is the region that suffers the most from the legacy of slavery and the lack of serious investment in infrastructure and education. The most arid interior does not add to favourable conditions for the region. Struck by famine and rampant corruption, ancient oligarchic structures perpetuate, turning the hard-working people of Mogananga into easy prey to exploitation by capitalists and conversion by the Tabernacles.

This region is the one that benefits the most from populist policies, but mostly pays for its debts. Investment in infrastructure here has the most social impact, but likely the least economic return (at least during the play window).

The region received only proper state attention during the military government, that ivested in proper infrastructure in Mognagna's cities. Though not popular at the time due cuts in purely assistential programs, the legacy infrastructure makes people in Mognagna saudosist of the dictatorship times.

### Economic Reality

<b>Cotton and textile.</b> Classic post-slavery economy in arid regions — the same land and labor structures that ran on enslaved people transition into exploitative sharecropping and later wage labor in cotton fields and small textile mills. It would explain the oligarchic persistence (same families, different legal arrangements) and creates a natural tension with modernization: the textile industry could be upgraded with education and investment, but the landlords benefit from keeping labor cheap and dependent.

<b>Livestock in the semi-arid scrubland.</b> Smaller-scale than Tapira's cattle operations, more subsistence-oriented, with a culture of small ranchers who are economically dependent on the large landowners for water rights and access to markets. This creates a different political texture than Tapira — not a monolithic agrobusiness bloc, but a diffuse network of small producers who are individually powerless and collectively captured.

### Political Landscape

This coastal state saw some share of trade and exposure to new ideas and was not as shielded from socialist ideals as Tapira. Thus, PM and CV have a large electoral base here, but the successful PM here was the populist façade, not a desenvolvimentist one, and a CV would only be powerful if it showed real action. FFF somewhat thrives, but proper legislation on churches or incentives may alter significantly this landscape. Neither CA or CP figure well with Mognagnians, but that could change, should a proper oligarchic structure  be fomented here as in Tapira, CP could gain strength; if real technological advancement brought real change, it could give CA some space.

### Relationship with the Capital

Central government is the sole responsible for keeping famine away from Mognagna and neither liberals, socialists, nor nationalists dared to changes on assistentialist programs there.

### Arcs involving Mognagna

- The natural betterment of education on the economy and CoL can improve EO of this region, and education may also provide opportunities to modernization, but the returns will be smaller than anywhere else.
- There should be a ceiling to CoL due to the natural harshness of the arid lands. To overcome this, the player may choose paths to either:
  - Invest strongly in an infrastructure project on <b>Desalination and water cistern networks.</b> i
  - promote migration to other regions, potentially causing loss of opinion in Mognagna, temporary loss of stability in all regions involved, and reducing education and infrastructure in receiving regions
- Another cap that must be tackled is the Coronels, oligarchs who have informal control over people. They can be mitigated by:
  - break them through land reform and judicial investment (expensive, politically costly nationally because Tapira's bloc fights it, but permanently raises the CoL ceiling and shifts the political landscape toward PM/CV)
  - ignore them (status quo, CoL ceiling stays, but approval holds because nobody feels threatened)
  - Empower and compromise them, potentially deepening the ceiling, but guaranteeing political support
- Again, cultural festivals and beach tourism can be invested in (and the second can be negotiated with capitalists instead of government investment). Culture here can make other regions less prone to racism.
