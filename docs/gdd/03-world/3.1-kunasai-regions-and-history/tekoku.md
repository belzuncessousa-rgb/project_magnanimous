## Lore

### Identity & Character

A series of factors makes the people of Tekoku feel as if they are distinct from the rest of the country. During most of the colonial period, it enjoyed prosperity due to being the main trade region of the continent. Both Rivers Arvane and Ibira cross the continent and end there; those were the main trade routes not only for Kunasai, but also from neighboring nations. It's flatlands favored the establishment of cities and monasteries, which from early on formed a species of intellectual elite in the region. Also contributing to the regional balance, it can be added that the main exploitation did not happen there, but in the southern provinces. Thus, slavery was not a strong institution there, nor did it suffered much from the chaotic process of abolition, still responsible for inequity and poor city services in other regions.

Besides that, later waves of immigration from Veldra due to the Great Wars in the old continent. All that contributes to the region's strong Veldran identity, transparent in the region's festivals, architecture, and even the language spoken in some countryside towns.

### Economic Reality

<i>What drives the economy day-to-day? Who benefits, who is excluded, what are the fault lines?</i>

<p>
Economy in Tekoku is twofold. A reasonable fruit and bovine production in the countryside, and a developing industrial and services. Since it is the most well-educated region in Kunasai, a disproportional tech and financial companies are based here, besides being home to more technological companies such as Strawberry (a national electronics brand) and Tekoku Money (the largest investment bank in Kunasai). 
</p>

That said, Tekoku faces serious challenges due to the drain of talent by both Veldra and Moreya and also the fast-growing neighboring region of Ibicuí. It must also be said that businesses suffer from heavy taxation and internationally can't really compete with Veldran, Moreyan, and Zharan technologies.

### Political Landscape

<p>
Tekoku is the base for both NF and CA, and for the recent elections, has been a steady political base for both. Both will compete strongly for the control of the electoral base or form a coalition for the next election. The flow of national affairs may favor one or the other. 
</p>

The litterate population could also be carefully steered to embrace some sort of socialism or equality policies as long as the quality of life is going well. CP could also find some electorate due to it's resemblance with CA, but between the two, it is likely Tekokunians would choose CA.

<p>
Though somewhat religious, the population of Tekoku is not likely to go with FFF due to their evangelical tendencies. 
</p>

The radical left CV can find only a handful of supporters, usually university students.

### Historical Wounds

<p>
Though not completely true, Tekoku has a frequent discourse that their taxes are used in populist policies in Ibira and Mognangna. There is also a latent racism in a part of the population, which could be directed to believe that the reason those states stall the country's development is due to being mainly comprised of indigenous and Ondaran descendants. 
</p>

Though not averse to totalitarian regimes, Tekoku strongly resents the military dictatorship. Both because it was the government that invested the most in infrastructure for underdeveloped provinces, and the intellectuals due to the military oppression and lack of free press.

### Relationship with the Capital

Though it possesses a strong regional irredentism, Tekoku considers itself an integral part of the nation, though it believes it should have more representation in the central government.

### Arcs involving Tekoku

- Very low approval and stability can trigger a path of separatism in Tekoku. This separatism could be supported by Mondrath if their opinion is too low or their aggressiveness too high
- During the two runs that the game is designed to cover, Tekoku should have paths to become a technological powerhouse, spinning off powerful technological companies or housing great powers' companies' headquarters, or even both (depending on investments in the region and international relations)
- Should conservationalism be left unchecked, the rivers that end in Tekoku could suffer, straining commerce passing through the region or bringing pollution that could damage the economy/wellfare
- Investments in agropecuary in this region could have potentialized returns if combined with trade deals with Zharan and diminished returns if regional deals with Arenza and Mondrath
- Technology, higher education, and the successful exploration of rare minerals in Ibira are also a potential combo
- Brain drain crisis / talent retention: drain to Veldra and Moreya as an economic challenge but it doesn't have a story arc yet. A slow deterioration path where inaction causes Strawberry or Tekoku Money to relocate or shrink, compounding into an education→economy spiral. The interesting design space is that the player can address this through economic investment (keep talent domestically competitive), through diplomacy (negotiate terms with Veldra that make emigration less attractive), or through cultural/identity policy (make Tekoku feel like the place to <i>be</i> — which ironically could push the region toward NF). Three very different levers, different compass costs.
- Electoral reform grieviance: Tekoku believes it deserves more representation. This is currently a background attitude, but it could have an arc: a constitutional reform moment where Tekoku's political class pushes for rebalancing representation, possibly in coalition with CA or NF. Lara can support it (gains approval in Tekoku, loses it in underrepresented regions like Mognagna or Ibira who'd lose relative weight), block it (stable elsewhere, Tekoku hardens against her), or redirect it into a decentralization package that partially satisfies everyone and fully satisfies no one. Classic reform trap
- Strawberry competition: If Lara deepens ties with either superpower, their tech companies will want market access — which directly threatens Strawberry. This could run as a quiet subplot: foreign pressure to open the tech market, Strawberry lobbying back, Tekoku's business elite split between those who benefit from foreign investment and those threatened by it. At a certain compass accumulation toward either superpower, it forces a decision — protect the national champion (ECON interventionist, Tekoku business community fractured) or open the market (ECON free-market, short-term economic gain, long-term Strawberry decline and potential relocation of HQ out of Kunasai entirely).
- The immigration question: Tekoku has a strong Veldran-heritage identity and a latent racism that currently points outward (toward Ibira/Mognagna). But Tekoku is also the most economically dynamic region — which means it probably attracts internal migration from poorer regions. There's a natural arc where demographic change <i>within</i> Tekoku becomes a flashpoint: Ondaran or indigenous internal migrants settling in Tekoku cities, straining services, activating the dormant racism vector from below rather than from above. NF could exploit this independently of Lara's choices, forcing her to respond. The interesting wrinkle is that restricting internal migration is constitutionally and politically toxic everywhere else — so whatever she does here has national costs.
- Investing in Veldran festivals or museums: increase Cultural Power and may decrease brain drain. It costs, of course, money, but has little downside other than that. Veldran festivals deepen irredentism, while museums may increase education slightly
