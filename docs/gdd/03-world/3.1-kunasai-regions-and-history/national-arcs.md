# National Arcs

Story support guides

<ul>
<li>
<b>The Constitutional Representation Crisis</b> - The three senators per state system may misrepresent both population and economy of some regions. Brought forth by Ibicuí elites this will be central crisis for this government: Tekoku wants more representation; Mognagna and Ibira are overrepresented relative to their economic weight; Ibicuí is underrepresented relative to its population and GDP. This is a genuine zero-sum national fight. A reform push forces the president to take a side: rebalance toward population/economy (gains in Tekoku and Ibicuí, alienates the poor regions whose political weight protects their welfare programs) and further decide the weight of the voting system (GDP or population), defend the status quo (protects the poor regions, hardens the wealthy ones against her), or attempt a decentralization compromise that devolves power regionally instead of rebalancing the center.
</li>
<li>
<b>The debt question - </b>inheriting a serious debt from previous governments, the interest mounts around 11% of initial revenue. At chapter 2, the player may choose to start paying the principal, borrow more, or maintain status quo. At chapter 5 the player may again choose to borrow more (with conditions based on current relations and debt/revenueratio), maintain status quo, or now start to pay the principal.  
</li>
<li>
<b>The great projects</b> - from six options, the player should be (somewhat) comfortably able to choose one at chapter two and another at chapter 4, but could sacrifice other things to choose more, up to 4.
