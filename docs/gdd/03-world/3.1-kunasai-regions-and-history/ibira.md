## Lore

### Identity & Character

Ibira is the most untamed region of Kunasai. Its dense rainforest makes it hard to implement serious infrastructure, and with recent conservation movements, makes it even more delicate to do so. The exploitation of Kunasai was conducted mainly through the enslavement of local indians and expeditions through the Ibira River and its tributaries.

Thus, only a handful of semi-successful settlements exist today, with the economy focused primarily on subsistence agriculture and fishing and the extraction of natural wood and plants.

The weak reach of government and the difficulty of coverage through the rainforest make it a prime target for smuggling and trafficking operations, with smuggler runs often being more developed than official roads and enabling cartels to conduct their international operations.

### Economic Reality

Mainly a subsistence economy, fishing, and foraging are what keep Ibira from starvation. A few legacies of rubber barons, often inherited by cartel leaders, are the sparse signals of ostentation in the cities.

With pressure from the northern neighbor Tapira to deforestation to make room for bovine and large plantations, and with potential mining operations that may bring some economic boom to Ibira at the severe risk of environmental degradation.

Nevertheless, nature is still thriving here and the potential to explore animal sightseeing, sportive fishing, waterfall trekking, and other ecotourism is latent should the place have enough stability and infrastructure.

### Political Landscape

The forgotten population of Ibira can only find solace in the speech of strong welfare parties, such as CV and PM and, should they focus on Kunasaian nationalism instead of Veldran irredentism, NF.

The miserable situation could also propel FFF. CA and CP on the other side are extremely unpopular, especially due to their said connection to the cartels.

### Historical Wounds

This region is so used to being forsaken by the state that its inhabitants barely have strong feeling towards it at all.

### Relationship with the Capital

Central state authority is not properly questioned when the state decides to intervene, the masses are politically disengaged while the Cartel and the small oligarchic elites appreciate the non-interventionism of the state.

### Arcs involving Ibira

Returns from this region may vary greatly, as there are multiple obstacles to tackle (crime, infrastructure, and at least an economic investment to make everything be economically worth investing), and even so, the return will not be astronomical. The idea here is to make the player feel the challenge and ponder not only the economy, but themes like national integration, environmental responsability, ethics, and social responsibility. Though dificult it should hopefully be fun, it is a game after all

- Infrastructure: the decadent and sorry state of Ibira's infrastructure must be addressed if any development and control is to be seriously successful in Ibira (tripling the results should infrastructure be developed), multiple infrastructures should have diminishing returns:
  - Fluvial, this will link this region with the rest of the country since the Ibira river starts here and crosses Tapira, Ibicuí, and ends in Tekoku. This could benefit all regions, especially industry-related investments. This should be the most expensive project
  - Airports in Ibira: To counter the difficulty of moving around the rainforest, airports and developing cities could be established. This sits well with tourism investments, though has less returns to integration and industry
  - Roads: neither cheap nor effective, this just increases infrastructure a bit
  - Railway network: also somewhat effective for indutry and integration, could cost the same as airports but with effective less returns. The only way to improve the return from this is to also make a deal with Tarev and Zharan to connect Ibira to ports sponsored by Zharan in Tarev.
- Cartels: a lot can happen with cartels on this region:
  - If stability is low and cartel demands are not met, this could trigger a mid-game crisis where there is an active fight with the cartels in the region.
  - Cartels could also be appeased by giving them more liberty and concessions, making them stronger with time, and damaging relations with neighbors and with the federation. It must be noticed that the player will have a chance to reduce cartel violence by maintaining the cartels, which should be the easiest option early on, but charge later on (demanding fees and corruptions scandal potential)
- If the average national education is high enough or if foreign concessions to the global powers were given, the exploration of rare minerals can be initiated (with different rights and costs if technological help from foreigners is needed). The returns will change based on infrastructure. When investing, the player may also choose to operate with minimal environmental impact, only if the education path was used, with the cost being smaller the higher the education. (this will only be able to trigger late 1st term, since it needs time for education and infrastructure to kick in)
- Cultural: investments in both cultural and ecological tourism can be made in Ibira. They have a small synergy bonus if they are to both be made, since it makes coming to Ibira more interesting. Ecotourism has fewer returns if investments that degrade nature are made.
- Concessions to Tapira farmers can also be made here, seriously degrading the environment, but appeasing them and creating more immediate income
- Indigenous people's relations could be connected to preservation, low religious pressure, and thheir rights that should be voted in congress and the player may endorse or refuse.
