# 3.2 Political Parties & Social Factions

## Political Parties

| Party | Acronym | Spectrum | Electoral base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| National Front | NF | Right-nationalist, identitarian, military-nostalgic | Tekoku, Mognagna |
| Capital Party | CP | Center-right economic + ruralist/agribusiness | Tapira, Ibicuí (elite), Ibira (agro) |
| Popular Movement | PM | Left progressive, redistributive, anti-militarist | Ibicuí (urban), Ibira (indigenous/environmental) |
| Communist Vanguard | CV | Far-left, radical, pressures PM from the left | Ibicuí (periphery), Ibira (radical movements) |
| Civic Alliance | CA | Technocratic, anti-corruption in rhetoric — elitist, advocates literacy-based voting restrictions | Ibicuí, Tekoku (educated class) |
| Faith and Family Front | FFF | Tabernacle conservative — moral agenda, populist in style | Mognagna, Ibicuí (periphery) |
| Order and Progress Party | OPP | Pure transactional — available to any government willing to pay the price | Capital, Ibicuí |

---

## Coalition dynamics

- <b>NF + CP:</b> Natural right-wing alliance, but NF distrusts CP's pragmatism
- <b>CP + FFF:</b> Cultural conservatism bridges them, but FFF's populism unnerves CP
- <b>PM + CV:</b> Ideological siblings who despise each other's tactics
- <b>CA:</b> Pivot party — can lean either direction; accepting their alliance always has a democratic cost
- <b>OPP:</b> Available to any government willing to pay the price — appointments, budget, local favors. Most reliable legislative partner and most politically embarrassing one to admit needing. Accepting OPP support moves the compass slightly toward Opportunist and gives ammunition to idealist characters.

<b>No coalition is clean. Every government is a compromise with something uncomfortable.</b>

## Social Factions

| Faction | Want | Fear | Natural alignment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Armed Forces | Institutional autonomy, budget protection, impunity for dictatorship-era crimes | Left government opening archives, prosecuting officers | NF; tolerates CP |
| The Communion | Institutional privileges, influence over education | Tabernacle growth eroding base, CA secularism | CP; uneasy with FFF |
| Media (3–4 family conglomerates) | Regulatory stability, access to power | Nationalization, content regulation | Center; hostile to CV |
| Business sector | Predictability, profit, trade agreements | Instability, radical tax reform, CV in power | CP, CA; NF (protectionist wing) |
| Unions | Consultation, labor protection | CP in power, labor reform weakening their structure | PM; radical wing in CV |
| Indigenous Movement | Territorial rights, autonomy in Ibira | Agribusiness, cartels, absent State | PM, CV |
| Landless Movement | Agrarian reform in Tapira and Ibira | CP, agribusiness, military | PM, CV |
| Cartels | Space to operate, impunity, weak State | Coordinated repression, international cooperation | None — corrupt across all parties |

---

## Cartels — design notes

- Native to Kunasai — product of inequality and state abandonment, not foreign import
- Some large enough to have foreign bases, creating diplomatic complications when repression is attempted
- Operate heavily in Ibira and peripheries of Ibicuí and Mognagna
- Intertwined with indigenous territories in Ibira — communities sometimes resist, sometimes are coerced
- <b>Corruption vector:</b> Different in nature from party corruption. Not ideology — money and threat. Player can refuse, accept occasionally, or become systematically dependent. Each level has distinct consequences on Suspicion indices and compass
- Active plots throughout: territorial control, rare earth extraction, international pressure on Kunasai to act (which means ceding sovereignty)
