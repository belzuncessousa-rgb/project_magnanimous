# Ministries & Cabinet

Government ministries: their standing themes, budget-funded initiatives, and the minister who runs each.

Each path should have a visit where it can be decided which effect should be the focus of the decision (increasing the effect under Effects). E.g. for Defense Minister. In the opening ceremony, a part of the discourse includes saying: “This Institute will bring us to the forefront of global ballistic technology.” Increasing the focus in education, or “We must focus on the practical effects for civil society,” increasing GDP increase and maybe moving DIPL -1.

## How ministries work

- The <b>term-1 cabinet is fixed</b> (formed by the pre-election coalition). The player builds their own cabinet only in term 2.
- At <b>Chapter 2</b> the player sets each ministry's <b>budget tier</b>. Tier determines how many initiatives that ministry runs:

| Tier | Initiatives | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cut | 0 | No initiative. FC freed up. |
| Low | 1 | Player picks which one — the meaningful tradeoff. |
| Regular | 2 | Both standard initiatives run. |
| High | 3 | Only ministries with a third high-tier initiative (Development, Defense, Law & Order). |

- Each funded tier costs <b>FC per turn</b> (recurring). Tier costs differ per ministry.
- A <b>mid-term re-budget</b> occurs alongside the debt meeting, letting the player adjust.
- <b>Standing themes</b> fire regardless of budget — they are the ministry's baseline reality.
- <b>Minister mood</b> reacts to the player's choices only (never to other ministers). Displeasing a minister lowers IPI and affects which factions will join a coalition for the second election.
