# Vice President

The Vice President is a distinct political power center, not a budget ministry. Where ministries <b>fund and build</b> (currency: FC, output: indices), the VP <b>legislates</b> — shepherding divisive bills through Congress. The VP's currency is political capital and congressional support, not budget.

## How the VP works

- The VP owns <b>legislative campaigns</b>: the player proposes a bill, factions line up for or against based on their compass and redlines, and the bill <b>passes</b>, <b>fails</b>, or <b>passes watered-down</b> depending on whether the player can assemble the votes.
- Legislative fights are the main place the <b>second-term coalition ledger</b> gets written — every bill makes factions remember whether the president was with them or against them.
- The VP is a <b>character with succession and ambition</b>: a loyal VP is an enforcer for the president's agenda; a VP who disagrees with the president's drift is a rival who may contest or inherit power in term 2. The VP can be the institutional brake on an extreme drift — or the partner who enables it.
- Detailed <b>chains of events</b> for each legislative question will be authored later in <b>6. Scenes</b>. The sections below describe them in text only.

## Legislative Questions

Each is its own chain of events (authored later in Scenes). Land reform and religious legislation are <b>perennial</b> fights the player can initiate when they dare; AI legislation is <b>event-triggered</b>, surfacing mid-game when a related arc fires.

### Land Reform <i>(player-initiated)</i>

Cuts the economic / oligarchic axis — the through-line connecting Tapira's landlords, Mognagna's Coronéis, and Ibira's exploited interior. The single most consequential domestic-structural fight in the world. Passing it raises CoL ceilings in the poor regions, breaks clientelist capture, and strongly pleases CV/PM; the landlord bloc and its Congressional allies (across Tapira, eastern Ibicuí, Mognagna) fight it to the death, and CA sides with property. The hardest bill to pass and the most transformative if it succeeds. Has a partial-success state: a watered-down reform that buys some CoL without truly breaking the oligarchy.

### Religious Legislation <i>(player-initiated)</i>

Cuts the RELIG / cultural axis, where FFF and the evangelical-church networks (strongest in Mognagna and Tapira) are the immovable object. Direction matters: secularizing (curriculum, church taxation, restricting political speech from the pulpit) versus accommodating (faith-based programs, moral legislation). Ibira (RELIG −8) and cosmopolitan Ibicuí cheer secularization; Mognagna and the FFF base revolt. Secretly linked to land reform: because the churches are instruments of the Coronéis, secularizing legislation is also covertly an anti-oligarchic move — two levers against the same power structure.

### AI Legislation <i>(event-triggered)</i>

Cuts the future / economic-modernization axis and is the most novel question. Touches Tekoku's tech sector and Strawberry, foreign tech-market pressure (the superpowers want their AI in the market), labor displacement (feeding the strike arcs), and the surveillance question that connects to Intelligence's Domestic Monitoring track and seeds the technocratic-dystopia ending. Has no natural constituency yet — the political system hasn't sorted itself around it — so the player has unusual freedom to define the terms, and whichever way they go <i>creates</i> the alignment rather than responding to one. Surfaces via a triggering event (Strawberry lobbying, a foreign AI product entering the market, or an automation layoff).
