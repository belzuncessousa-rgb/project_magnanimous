## Foundational creative direction

- Political narrative RPG inspired by Suzerain and other strategy games, with a distinctive Kunasaian identity, substantial economic/political mechanics, regional inequality, political bargaining and interlocking arcs. Grounded, occasionally funny, nuanced, never preaching a preferred ideology. A complex world should not require a complicated reading experience. Introduce names and facts when relevant, remind players naturally, provide optional depth, and make immediate dilemmas understandable. Avoid bureaucratic monologues, robotic dialogue, aphoristic/antithesis tics and repetitive menu-like meetings; retain substantive policy discussion. Give NPCs independent relationships and interests, ordinary life and distinct voices.
- **Player character: fixed biography, player-defined motives, feelings, personality and ideology.** Do not assume Lara was compassionate, honest, reluctant to use power, devoted to the poor, or privately agreed with her own public words. Her reputation is other people's perception, not narrative truth. The same legal/community career is compatible with altruism, ambition, resentment, opportunism, capitalism, communism, corruption, etc. Do not impose internal thoughts or emotions; allow optional thought choices and changes of mind, and distinguish public speech from private belief. Apply the same principle to future playable presidents. Existing "the voice" and path_hold/path_corrupt/path_harden morality framing need review; not a commitment to remove the mechanics. Avoid forced moral labels on ambiguous choices.
- No single correct ideology. Distinguish policy orientation from actual outcomes, public record from personal success, and convictions from tactics. Preserve invisible compass/indirect feedback where appropriate. Decisions should have tradeoffs and consequences, not predetermined moral verdicts.
- **Scope:** ship Lara Vento's complete first four-year term. Current implemented chapters 1–3 (year one) are a playable prototype, not the final narrative. First term must feel complete but establishes conditions for a potential second term (sequel/expansion undecided). Lucas Belzens and Edvar Solano remain potential future playable presidential campaigns; reserve those without multiplying v1 writing. Alternate winners have a common historical foundation with plausible changed election outcomes and starting arrangements. Moderate outcomes can overlap; character-specific political networks and legitimacy enable distinctive radical futures.
- Lara's particular radical possibilities include revolutionary communism and popular personalist/caudillista authoritarianism. NPC Lucas is liberal/modernizing and CA-adjacent; future playable Lucas may pursue radical capitalism or technocratic oligarchy (including educational voting qualifications). NPC Solano moves toward Veldran purism; future playable Solano can pursue different directions, including militarist/nationalist authoritarianism. Lara and Solano have stronger natural access to personalist authoritarian paths than Lucas. These are political opportunities, not fixed player personalities. First term may empower the CV and create conditions for revolution rather than enact a full revolution already.
- Meaningful gated choices: investments, institutional strength, prior policies, relationships and preparation should unlock new choices, not just stat improvements. Show available choices only by default. Example for later design: a president with sufficiently empowered and loyal intelligence institutions and military cooperation could have a deceptive alternative to genuine military archive disclosure. Specific requirements and mechanics not yet defined. Keep consequences and feasibility coherent, avoid combinatorial explosion.

## 01 — Era and technology

Late 2010s / early 2020s equivalent (working reference 2018–2022, not a literal year). Smartphones, social media, online commerce, digital banking, modern industry and semiconductor research coexist with outdated infrastructure and inequality. AI is emerging, not post-2024 near-futuristic technology. Include a minor legislative/Senate debate on AI; possible connection to Tekoku/Strawberry, research, employment and privacy, not a major AI plot. Cybersecurity and surveillance exist but keep period-appropriate capabilities.

## 02–03 — Identity and ethnic composition

Kunasai has a real shared national identity. Inequality between states, not merely absolute poverty, sparks ethnic/cultural strife. This term is a turning point for national identity and a central arc. Allow the player to steer toward distinctly Kunasaian nationalism, discriminatory Veldran purism, progressive cosmopolitanism, or inclusive/moderate patriotism without prejudice. Nationalism and Veldran supremacy are distinct; the IDEN axis alone may not express both (separate narrative flags are a proposal). Avoid treating ancestry as destiny. Use regional inequality, migration, representation, St. Veldra naming debate, indigenous rights, curricula, cultural policy and NF mobilization as possible intersections. Cultural Power should be meaningful; exact effects undecided.

Regional mixture is confirmed: Ibicuí heavily intermixed and metropolitan/cosmopolitan; Tekoku more distinct Veldran heritage; Ibira strong indigenous communities alongside settlers and mixed populations. Working extrapolations: Mognagna intermixed, rural/local/religious traditions; Tapira landowning families alongside agricultural/industrial communities. Precise demographics open; no region homogeneous. Existing colonial history (Veldra, Ondaran enslavement, indigenous survival) remains unless revised.

## 04 — Religion

Predominantly religious society. Religion has an optional but meaningful arc, more natural political leverage for Solano but accessible to Lara. The Communion is traditional and institutionally established; the Tabernacle is rapidly growing, charismatic, rooted particularly in poorer communities, inspired by the social dynamics of Brazilian evangelical movements. Many senior pastors are corrupt and exploit faith and economic vulnerability for enrichment and power; ordinary believers and individual local clergy can be sincere, and the movement provides real community support. Do not caricature every believer. Secular movements exist, particularly in educated/progressive circles.
Player can promote secularism, contain/investigate/regulate the Tabernacle alone, cooperate with the Communion against it, join/ally with the Tabernacle and FFF, or largely ignore religion. Growth of FFF and Tabernacle continues independently of the president. Details of events and mechanics later.

## 05–06 — Dictatorship and democratic history

Declining democratic trust allows military-rule nostalgia to become publicly acceptable again. Older citizens remember conflicting experiences (development/jobs/order versus repression); younger people may romanticize a regime they did not experience. Existing Amnesty Act and military archive conflict retained. The armed forces seek protection of institutional autonomy and historical immunity. The player's actions, institutional investments and prior alliances can create alternative resolutions. Do not lock specific archive mechanics yet.

Democracy had genuine accomplishments. First two presidents made meaningful progress (not necessarily morally good individuals). A respected president immediately before Tercilio Luz successfully handled a difficult economic period, improved the country substantially and left office without major scandals; inspired by the idea of successful macroeconomic stabilization, not a copy of a real politician. Could still be alive and comment on current events. Not universally admired; unresolved structural inequality remains. Luz (PM) leaves overpriced unfinished sanitation works and debt; subsequent CP government discredited by bribery; Lara inherits stagnation, debt and loss of confidence. The cross-party investigation in Lara's history complicates this inheritance.

Author is open to pushing democratization further into the past to fit additional presidents. Working proposal: ~33 years of democracy, ~8 former presidents (rather than the prior 25 years/six presidents); NOT locked. Exact dates, names, terms and parties need reconciliation with regional lore, Lara's age/career and the dictatorship duration. Prior illustrative six-president chronology (25–20 transition, 20–16 public services, 16–12 instability, 12–8 successful stabilizer, 8–4 Luz, 4–0 CP) is superseded as a fixed timeline by this open question, but relative order of the last three stands. Avoid spending effort on every historical president.

## 07 — Presidential power and political capital

Strong constitutional presidency, with specific democratic safeguards introduced at redemocratization. These should be meaningful hurdles to constitutionally authoritarian paths; revolutionary transformation may instead replace the constitutional order. Distinguish democratic leadership, constitutional authoritarianism, personalist authoritarianism and revolution. Specific powers, checks and reform procedures TBD.
Author wants to revisit Claude's legislative/political-capital mechanics: political bargaining should be events/conversations with the parties to convince, not automatic vote purchases. Different parties have different convictions and interests; previous relationships, commitments, political capital and concessions should affect available arguments/agreements. Support can be earned through shared policy goals as well as transactions. Rework details when designing Congress and mechanics; do not change the engine yet.

## 08 — Social media

Author proposed a Twitter-like optional feed alongside newspapers, featuring recurring personalities (former president, celebrities, journalists, politicians, etc.), comments on current affairs and everyday nonpolitical life, and endorsements/photos with candidates closer to election. Not every post about Lara; give world independent life. Conditional reactions and foreshadowing can complement institutional newspaper coverage. Working proposals: 12–20 recurring accounts, low-volume irregular posting, reuse newspaper-like content conditions/portraits. Name ("Pulse" was an example), exact UI/implementation and account roster not locked; coordinate with art/UI work.

## 09 — Personal wealth

Implement a distinct personal money/wealth dimension, independent of FC. Wealth must have meaningful scale and uses besides accumulation. Personal wealth may be used to help family, fund causes or political activities, and purchase a comfortable or luxurious lifestyle. A sufficiently wealthy president may have an ending in which she leaves Kunasai after election loss or impeachment and lives luxuriously abroad. Country outcome and president's personal outcome should be independent. Keep lightweight narrative mechanics, not a lifestyle simulation. Working proposals: PW resource, threshold-based opportunities, salary, legitimate investments, gifts, corruption/kickbacks as possible sources, campaign support and charitable donations as uses. Exact units, mechanics and thresholds TBD; no changes to resource system yet.

## 10–12 — Lara's personal life and family

Lara is 42 at inauguration. A loving but imperfect working-class family in Ibicuí; both parents and siblings are desirable and adopted for design. Family members have different attitudes toward life, work, opportunity, inequality, religion and responsibility, not necessarily explicitly partisan. Let the player define her feelings toward family and upbringing. Working proposal: father 69, mother 65, brother 47, younger sister 33 (ages other than Lara provisional). Father proud of independence/hard work; mother sociable, religious, generous; brother made a comfortable middle-class life, perhaps small business, dislikes bureaucracy; sister educated but professionally struggling, frustrated by inequality/nepotism. These are starting sketches, not final profiles. Give contradictions and ordinary family scenes. Avoid forcing the whole family into political allegory. Personal wealth, public attention and potential nepotism can intersect with family life.
Amanda Troes is a longtime friend from university and an exceptionally competent, serious, honest public servant genuinely motivated to improve lives; embodies the kind of president the public expected Lara to be. Retain this relationship. Amanda should have independent goals, life, humour and fallibility; potential conflict between personal loyalty and principles. Possible outcomes friendship, disagreement, estrangement, complicity or resignation (not all locked). Do not turn her into a constant moral narrator. Reconsider Claude's existing Celia/Marco storylines; names/roles can be kept, changed or replaced. Single/childless Lara is a working assumption, not explicitly locked. Romance not decided. Existing Celia illness, father absence, Marco role and "voice" plot must not constrain the redesign.

## 13–14 — Lara's career and investigation

Confirmed: studied law, meets Amanda at university; becomes a public prosecutor; provides legal aid through an NGO in Ibicuí alongside legal career (not necessarily primary employment). Longstanding informal PM connections during legal career; joins formally after leaving prosecution, serves as senator, then president at 42. Her motivations are always up to the player. Working age timeline: studies 18–23, legal career/NGO 23–35, national fame 35–37, enters formal politics 37–38, senator 38–42. Adjust if needed.
National fame comes from a major **cross-party corruption investigation**, involving government officials, contractors and business leaders. Genuine wrongdoing uncovered and convictions obtained, but network survives; some people remain influential, including possible allies. Links to both older administrations, PM's own corruption, returning legal contacts, future investigations and media scrutiny. Do not decide whether her motive was justice or ambition. Potential prior professional connection with Renata Salgado to be explored. Exact case chronology, evidence, targets and cast not defined. Reveal layers progressively through scenes rather than requiring players to memorize the whole network.

## 15–16 — Elections, rivalry and party evolution

Lara wins comfortably (working runoff result 56–44 against Solano); no congressional majority. Future Lucas/Solano campaigns must be plausible alternative election outcomes; narrower wins possible, Solano potentially harder due to more extreme starting circumstances. No exact alternate results locked. Difficulty through political circumstances and institutional resistance rather than arbitrary handicaps.
Lucas and Solano are the two principal national rivals; no additional major presidential rival needed. Both have independent objectives and can cooperate with Lara on specific issues. Rival strength and opposition evolve with regional compasses, approval, economic performance, party relationships and policies. Final election may be won by any of the three. Retain regional compass and approval mechanics; player can adapt her own compass, change regional political preferences, or improve popularity through economic success.
Final chapter focuses on reelection, securing party leadership/candidacy, new affiliations and alliances, negotiating next-term cabinet composition/positions alongside policy commitments, and campaigning. These possibilities must depend on prior governance, alliances and achievements. Preserve modular campaign speech as a strong starting point; final structure and election formulas TBD. Internal party leadership matters. Party splits, mergers or defections may occur conditionally; design authored arcs rather than unrestricted party simulation. Early endings include assassination and removal/impeachment; risk depends on antagonized groups, their capacity, institutional support and precautions, not merely extremism. Regular endings include election loss, voluntary retirement and reelection, with personal outcomes distinct. Detailed early-ending rules TBD.

## 17–23 — Parties

Keep identities legible. Do not give every party a full spectrum of factions. PM and CP warrant substantial internal tensions; CV and CA are relatively ideologically pure, others have clear motivations. Existing party leaders/bench leaders and seat counts remain working implementation until reviewed.

- **PM (Popular Movement):** first major left-wing party after democratization, genuinely ideological in origin but initially the main vehicle for diverse leftists and populists. Governing strengthened pragmatic elements. Proposed informal tendencies: democratic socialists, statists, popular pragmatists, radical socialists; consolidate if too many faces. Pelan Avros is congressional bench leader (statist, corruption connections); Lara is party leader; Luz former president. Lara may reshape PM, renew ideology, become pragmatic, ally with CV, personalize it or lose leadership. Populism can exist on left or right; distinguish it from ideological commitments.
- **CV (Communist Vanguard):** split from PM over compromises and capitalist institutions. Fundamentally revolutionary communist, currently using democratic means because it lacks the political power for revolution. Do not add artificial moderate/revolutionary faction split. A radical Lara can empower CV and set stage for second-term transformation.
- **CP (Capital Party):** powerful economically liberal/centre-right coalition of urban capitalists (industry, finance, technology, services) and agribusiness/older traditional economic establishment. NPC Lucas Belzens is more comfortable with urban interests and ideologically closer to CA but belongs to CP because CP can realistically elect a president; uneasy alliance with rural interests. Vando Trembolo represents agribusiness in existing cast. Conditional schism: Lara may exploit tensions to weaken CP, with agribusiness defecting to/merging with FFF. Consequence could be a powerful rural-religious bloc; exact triggers undecided.
- **CA (Civic Alliance):** ideologically pure knowledge/education/meritocracy party, not inherently capitalist. Believes qualified/educated people should govern, prioritizes universal education and research, competence and access to knowledge. Can steer toward elitist political participation restrictions (only educated citizens vote or have full political rights). Even radical CA values universal education as route to eligibility. Technocratic governance, research investment and credential-based institutions are possible directions, not all starting policies. Natural affinity with NPC Lucas.
- **NF (National Front):** officially Kunasaian nationalist, military strength, sovereignty, strong state, national pride. Strong Veldran cultural influence via Tekoku; NPC Solano gravitates toward Veldran purism. Party itself not initially Veldran supremacist. Player Solano remains free. Potential inclusive nationalist alternative for Lara; exact conditions for NPC shift TBD.
- **FFF (Faith and Family Front):** political instrument of powerful Tabernacle pastors primarily seeking wealth, privileges and political influence; ordinary electorate may genuinely embrace religious/moral agenda. Leadership flexible on economic and other policies when deals protect interests and public credibility. May absorb CP agribusiness faction. Distinct from ideologically pure CV/CA and less openly transactional than OPP.
- **OPP (Order and Progress Party):** emerged during democratization from experienced politicians, regional power brokers and establishment figures. No presidential ambitions; aims to remain indispensable to any president through patronage, appointments, resources and influence. Prefers dependent governments; publicly defends governability/pragmatism/stability. Reliable when agreements honoured. Mertens is not inherently a scheming would-be president; he represents the coalition's interests. Retain previous OPP red lines and coalition setup pending detailed redesign.

## 24 — Representation crisis

Equal state representation (three senators per state) was a deliberate founding compromise of democratization to protect smaller/poorer states. Existing populations: Ibicuí 100M, Mognagna 70M, Tekoku 50M, Tapira 30M, Ibira 30M. Equal Senate shares 20% each. Reform is a challenge to a democratic founding agreement, not simply correction of an obvious error. Ibicuí and Tekoku seek more representation; weaker regions fear losing institutional leverage and resources. Retain existing potential population-weight, GDP-weight and decentralization approaches from GDD as material to develop; exact procedure, powers and choices not finalized. Existing implementation treats ordinary Congress votes as unicameral and Senate as ratifier of federal pact changes; reconcile constitutional powers before writing this arc. Existing Senator Lessa as a face is provisional until character work.

## 25 — Federalism and the constitutional amendment package (PEC)

Confirmed: relatively centralized federation with elected governors, state legislatures/administrations, most tax revenue collected federally, substantial dependence on federal transfers and major projects, and wealthy states' resentment of redistribution. Exact fiscal and administrative competences still need design. Separatism is a potential arc, to be developed.

Author proposes incorporating Senate representation and regional autonomy into a broader cross-subject constitutional amendment package (working name PEC). The constitutional reform goes to the Senate, together with other major constitutional provisions. During actual party negotiations Lara can compromise on the contents and formulation of the amendment, alter or drop provisions desired by particular parties, or accept other demands as part of an agreement. Do not reduce this to an isolated representation choice or a generic purchase of votes.

Working implementation proposal, not final: modular draft composed of provisions, revised through authored negotiation scenes and visible for consultation between meetings. Potential subjects include Senate representation (equal/population/GDP), federalism, tax collection/transfers, executive powers, electoral eligibility/rules, institutional safeguards and cultural/regional rights. Illustrative bargaining positions only: CP fiscal autonomy, CV opposition to GDP weighting and interest in social guarantees, CA meritocratic administration, OPP protection of electoral influence and prosecution red lines, NF executive/military powers. Exact demands, interactions, gates, thresholds, timing, voting procedure, scope and consequences remain undecided; design together with the Congress overhaul. Separatism may motivate constitutional bargaining or be aggravated by its outcome. Do not imply the player must pass a PEC to complete the term.

## 26 — Tekoku separatism

Preserve the existing [Tekoku GDD](https://app.notion.com/p/36b10ad0215880c0a953e31afded3b35): initially Tekoku sees itself as an integral part of Kunasai, with strong regional identity and representation grievances; very low approval and stability can trigger separatism, and hostile/aggressive Mondrath may support it. Fiscal resentment, Veldran identity, latent racism, internal migration and representation disputes are existing contributing factors. Integrate this arc with the PEC rather than replacing the GDD. Confirmed author decision: actual Tekoku independence can occur within Lara's first term. Working proposal: exceptional accumulated crisis (or negotiated concession), escalating from discontent to political mobilization, constitutional confrontation and possible foreign involvement; potential outcomes reconciliation, autonomy, unresolved crisis or independence. Map, national fiscal/economic aggregation, legislature, elections, technology, diplomacy and later content must accommodate territorial loss. A separate diplomatic entity is a provisional implementation idea, not locked: the [Mondrath GDD](https://app.notion.com/p/36b10ad0215881818a8fc242bc2db107) already contemplates Tekoku seeking union with Mondrath. Exact secession outcomes, conditions, timing and mechanical design remain open. Consult relevant GDD pages before each new design question; do not ask the author to restate settled lore.

Tekoku can secede in the first term as an independent republic OR join Mondrath, depending on circumstances. ## 28 — Mognagna and the Coronels

Confirmed: independent regional power brokers with cross-party relationships, especially OPP and PM populists, rather than a single party faction. Retain the [Mognagna GDD](https://app.notion.com/p/36b10ad021588098b6f7d53292b43373) approaches: dismantle through land/judicial reform, tolerate, or empower/negotiate. Author explicitly welcomes critical editorial assessment of plausibility, fun, engagement and production cost, not merely agreement. Design critique/proposals: avoid obvious moral optimization (credible reasons even reform-minded presidents might negotiate with local brokers); reward costly social development with visible human/narrative changes as well as indices; show regional culture, historical significance and enterprise rather than defining Mognagna solely by deprivation. Consider a prominent locally respected Coronel whose patronage simultaneously sustains dependency and provides services/employment where institutions fail. Specific character and events not locked.

## 29 — Tapira, agriculture, and decision design

Author correction to session 28: moral complexity does not require morally ambiguous choices. Coronel clientelism is harmful; do not invent redeeming qualities or hidden moral downsides to justify it. Dismantling it is difficult because it consumes fiscal and political resources, threatens entrenched interests and risks power that could otherwise be used to benefit people. Retain existing GDD options. Some decisions can be morally ambiguous, others morally clear but strategically costly; do not overly relativize or complicate. Mognagna should also show its cultural and historical importance, economic potential, sizeable population and potential workforce for industrialization, not just poverty and corruption.

Confirmed Tapira design: preserve the [Tapira GDD](https://app.notion.com/p/36b10ad0215880008b6bd36cd310f8d9) and expand land reform/replacement of established elites as costly options. Distinguish independent but interacting development dimensions: (1) land distribution and political power — agrarian reform, maintaining oligarchic ownership, or further concentration; (2) agricultural technology and modernization, with environmental management affecting agricultural outcomes across ownership arrangements. No forced association between a particular land distribution and technological or environmental policy. Existing drought, bailout, transgenics, trade leverage and elite political influence remain relevant. The established agribusiness elite has genuine financial and political power, making attempts to break, reform or replace it costly. Working implementation idea: use existing education, infrastructure, economic output and environmental flags where feasible rather than unnecessary new indices; specific costs, effects and event sequences TBD. Matrix of possible combinations is conceptual, not six separate scripted paths.

## 30 — Ibira and national integration

Author direction: Ibira's exceptional natural wealth brings responsibility, risks and opportunities. Economic development should be optional, but neglect must not make the region irrelevant. Cartels remain a national problem even if the president pursues no economic investment there; conservation has cross-regional economic consequences. Preserve these interconnections and the existing [Ibira GDD](https://app.notion.com/p/36b10ad02158806cab49e548ea26b27d). Avoid an all-or-nothing resource sink or excessive separate arcs. Organize around three interacting strands: security/state authority (cartels, corruption, territorial control and nationwide consequences); environmental preservation (forest, rivers, biodiversity, downstream agriculture/commerce); optional economic development/integration (infrastructure, minerals, tourism, trade). Reward coordination and different commitment levels, not just total spending. Environmental and indigenous issues are distinct but can intersect.

Author delegated indigenous design: establish some legally recognized indigenous territories dating from democratization, with inadequately enforced boundaries and protections and unresolved claims. Focus on a limited number of meaningful decisions about territorial rights, consultation over concessions/projects, services/security, culture and religious freedom. Indigenous communities are not homogeneous or uniformly opposed to development. Integrate these into the three strands rather than a fourth large arc. Specific rights, characters and events remain provisional. Preserve infrastructure choices but differentiate their purpose: fluvial national integration, airports access/tourism, rail industry/minerals/international links, roads basic connectivity; no need for a long separate storyline for every investment. Exact spillovers, triggers, security and conservation mechanics remain undecided. Author encourages constructive disagreement, especially on gameplay engagement and scope.

## 31 — Cartel identities and outcomes

Confirmed distinction: Red Tree emphasizes territorial control, production and illegal extraction in remote/rural areas; its objective is freedom to operate with minimal state interference or accommodating agreements, NOT sovereignty, separatism or establishing a government. Blue Piranhas emphasizes financial power, trafficking, laundering and institutional influence, seeking protection and impunity. Both are violent criminal organizations with an uneasy truce. Preserve the [Cartels GDD](https://app.notion.com/p/37110ad0215881a89e42d93c32d25bf4), including nationwide consequences regardless of Ibira investment, cross-party corruption, Tarev connections and foreign cooperation. Author supports distinctive identities and asks that design be tested for engagement and implementability. Retain ability to defeat both or one; preserve potential cooperation with one against the other. Proposed outcomes include both defeated, either defeated, accommodation, or failure. Defeating one need not require cooperation with the other. Working implementation proposal: separate existing opinion, capacity-to-act and suspicion attributes for each, shared authored arc with conditional scenes and outcomes rather than independent campaigns or a criminal economy simulation. Exact mechanics, triggers, costs and definition of defeat remain open; downstream scene consistency is principal implementation risk. Do not assume eradication of all crime.

## 32 — First-term closure and second-term transformations

Confirmed: Lara can completely dismantle both Red Tree and Blue Piranhas as major organizations in the first term; this does not eliminate all crime. Each major first-term arc should reach a recognizable finale, even when unsuccessful. Surviving cartels may be substantially stronger and become a major problem for the next administration. Tekoku separatism and Mondrath remain distinct interacting arcs with possible first-term territorial loss. Distinguish arcs capable of definitive first-term resolution from progressive development arcs and naturally long-term transformations (cultural change, radical changes of government). Firm author constraint: democracy cannot end in the first term; a definitive regime transition belongs to the second term. First-term institutional weakening, consolidation of executive power and preparation for radical transformations remain possible, within that constraint. Assassination, removal from office and territorial loss remain possible first-term outcomes. Exact thresholds, arc finales, institutional safeguards and mechanics TBD. The conclusive/progressive/transformative taxonomy is a working editorial framework, not a rigid classification of every arc.

## 33 — Environmental policy and scene-level reactivity

Confirmed: environmental conservation is a comparatively subtle system, with gradual indirect and interconnected effects rather than its own mandatory national crisis. Preserve regional environmental arcs, including Ibira deforestation, downstream river pollution, Tapira agricultural vulnerability and Mognagna water scarcity. Earlier conservation/exploitation decisions change existing later events and economic outcomes. Accelerate real-world timescales sufficiently for meaningful four-year gameplay. Players may underestimate or ignore conservation; provide credible warning signs through reports, dialogue and news without revealing all consequences. Exact mechanics, magnitude and timings TBD.

### Author direction — extensive scene personalization

Broad outcomes must not be the only branching dimension. The same ending or major scene should have meaningful conditional participants, dialogue, relationships, references to earlier decisions and presentation details. Author example: corrupt wealthy Lara leaves a country in crisis to live in a tropical paradise; depending on earlier schemes and surviving relationships she might be alone, accompanied by the leader of Blue Piranhas or Red Tree, or other associates, sharing cigars and conversation. More than one relevant companion may appear. The exile ending is not restricted to cartel corruption; other corruption routes remain to be designed. Do not make companionship automatic merely because a deal occurred; account for survival, availability, relationship and circumstances. Do not impose Lara's private emotional interpretation. The example illustrates intended depth across the game, not just endings.

Working implementation approach: common scene foundations with conditional participants, passages, callbacks and visual assets rather than separate files for every combination. Invest especially in pivotal scenes (endings, cabinet, betrayals, reunions, negotiations and personal milestones); routine scenes still acknowledge relevant history. Track durable facts and relationships, use reusable condition predicates and test contradictory or missing states. Existing scene format and engine support must be audited before deciding implementation; no new feature implemented here. Aim for replayability and distinctive player stories without uncontrolled combinatorial growth.

## 34 — Foreign alignment and execution-dependent strategies

Confirmed: geopolitical alignment is accumulated through decisions and individual country relationships, not a fixed route selection. Lara can cultivate both superpowers simultaneously, and neutrality can be a successful active strategy. Attempts at alignment may fail: domestic policies incompatible with a prospective partner, conflicting commitments, poor relationships or weak execution may leave Kunasai without either superpower's support. Trading with both is generally less consequential than strategic commitments; exact thresholds and conditions TBD. General game principle: player chooses strategy, not guaranteed outcome. This applies across economic, political, social and diplomatic systems; do not reward ideological consistency alone or treat a chosen direction as automatic success. Existing foreign relational indices and compass remain the basis; reliability, leverage, agreements and compatibility are conceptual factors, not necessarily new tracked resources. Author welcomes multiple focused discussions within complex topics; do not mechanically limit every section to one question. Prioritize meaningful interaction and manageable implementation, then specify mechanics when needed.

## 35 — Superpower identities and demands

Confirmed: the Arennian Federation and Zharan are stylized, more sharply defined fictional analogues of the contemporary US–China rivalry, not literal copies. Both compete for ideological, economic and geopolitical dominance. The Federation emphasizes ideological compatibility, distribution of power, maintaining its sphere of influence and a paternalistic/imperial diplomatic attitude; it also pursues advantageous resource access and restrictions on Zharan's strategic/economic access. Zharan is diplomatically pragmatic and transactional, placing greater emphasis on economic demands, market access, trade, infrastructure and strategic concessions. Its risks include dumping, overly open domestic markets, cheap imports undermining domestic industries and accumulated dependency. These are priorities, not exclusive domains. Preserve cumulative alignment and potential failure from session 34. Proposed examples: affordable technology improving living standards while threatening Strawberry; infrastructure concessions accelerating Ibira development but creating obligations; agricultural exports increasing Tapira income and trade dependence. Both powers can offer genuinely beneficial agreements; avoid making either an obviously correct or uniformly exploitative partner. Exact agreements, pressure thresholds and consequences remain open. Sources: existing [Federation GDD](https://app.notion.com/p/36b10ad0215881e6a4f3fcb8ddcb33ff) and [Zharan GDD](https://app.notion.com/p/36b10ad0215881ef8853fa5963dc6170), whose detailed lore was largely TBD.

## 36 — Cold war, submission and diplomatic risk

Confirmed: no open international war during regular first-term gameplay, including no open war between the superpowers. Economic warfare, diplomatic confrontation, technological restrictions, espionage, threats and indirect rivalry remain possible; do not equate proxy pressure with open warfare. A war with the Archduchy of Mondrath may be a terminal first-term development/ending, not a playable military campaign. Dedicated war mechanics are deferred to a possible second-term game, not committed to scope.

Both superpowers are willing to push for Kunasai's submission, not merely mutually advantageous trade. Zharan's rapid rise and economic dominance should feel genuinely threatening despite its more pragmatic diplomatic manner. Careful, reasonably legible negotiation and compromise can produce mutual benefits and preserve autonomy; repeated concessions to either or both can produce effective foreign domination, potentially akin to recolonization despite formal sovereignty. Economic prosperity and sovereignty can diverge. ## Two budget allocations during Lara's first term

Before the first congressional negotiation, ministers brief Lara on inherited services, possible cuts and available initiatives. A working budget lets her simulate the changes and see what share of the projected surplus she needs. Routine negotiations usually yield a modest increase. A poor outcome can require a small reduction from inherited spending. Political organizing or concessions can secure a larger increase and create later obligations. Congress settles overall spending room. After the vote, Fontes presents debt service and the options to maintain the debt schedule, borrow finite additional funds, or dedicate some spending room to principal repayment. Carry debt and its consequences into the legacy assessment.

With Fontes, Lara sets overall allocations for Health and Social Programs, Education, Defense, Justice/Interior/Labour and Intelligence. She can redirect savings within or across ministries, including at zero net growth. Lara gives programme instructions to each minister within that allocation. Calls and meetings vary in length according to the decision. The signed budget folder records the allocations and programme decisions. Planning and Development handles individual investments, including Great Projects with possible staged payments. Exterior may have individual investments through diplomacy. Casa Civil coordinates the process.

Use the GDD ministry tables for each initiative, its prerequisites, regional effects, timing and potential actions. Each investment or cut should have a material effect on the simulation, the story, or both. Intelligence has the widest narrative ramifications. At unchanged funding Lara can pursue one of its two first-term tracks. An increase can deepen one track or support both at more limited depth. Unchanged Health funding maintains inherited services and gives no automatic new initiative. Standing events and crises can still bring Dara to Lara.

The final allocation takes place within the first term under existing congressional authority. Fontes shows free funds after remaining implementation payments and any continuing operating costs. Others have smaller recurring costs once established. Eligible unchosen initiatives can return.
