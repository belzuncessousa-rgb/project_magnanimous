# B · The delivery book, the rota and the waiting list

*Ch3–5 playable prose. Bracketed dates and scopes are substitutions from the responsible office's record. Show only eligible options. Read back cost, source, covered places, duration and displaced scope before any funding confirmation. A request for an estimate spends nothing.*

## 3.2 · The Dry Season

*Mognagna interior · mognagna-sertao.*

There are two lines outside the town hall. The one against the wall is for the tanker. A council employee walks along the other line, asking who has come to register.

Governor Julio Porper opens the meeting upstairs. He has brought the municipal requests and a note from the state transport office. He invites the mayor to start with the smaller roads.

> **Mayor** — “On the main road we can use the tanker. Past the bridge it has to be the small truck. We can send it twice if it comes back before the depot closes.”

Owin Dara opens the household register beside the transport note.

*Use the verified payment branch.*

- **Existing or extended run paid.** “The payment office confirmed this run on [payment date]. These households have received it. We can use the verified list to reach them again.” If eligibility was extended, identify the newly paid district or group. The mayor puts a finger on a village outside the list. “We still need someone to go there and take names.”
- **Run reduced, narrower or outstanding.** Dara reads the payment that cleared, the lower amount or the last paid date, as applicable. “I need the state figures for the places we can't reach on this list.” Porper checks if the regional contribution can cover intake and a first delivery. Where he has no credible offer he identifies the confirmed municipal capacity and the route still open for assessment.

*If verified, Dara gives the current clinic hours and any corroborated demand. At Sugar Bay use the staffed follow-up rota on its actual effective date, or the inherited rota. A scheduled appointment and an attended visit have separate records.*

> **Porper** — “The depot can tell you what it can carry this week. The towns will need to tell us who still needs it.”

**“Let me see what has arrived.”** At the depot the clerk brings the receiving book. Beside each load that has a signed receipt she has entered the date and receiving point. A resident waiting for a second trip asks when the small truck is coming. The clerk gives the next dispatch window if the driver confirmed it. **“Ask the town to check who received it.”** The clerk takes down the destination and sends the request. Return to the meeting.

**“Use the verified list for the first stage.”** *If there is a usable paid-recipient list and municipal capacity.* Dara costs expedited household assistance through that list and includes a separate intake for the outlying villages if it can be staffed. If capacity runs short, keep the villages on the uncovered list. This scopes the estimate and returns to the choice of response.

**“Can Defense reach the smaller roads?”** Dara asks the transport officer for compatible vehicles and crew dates. If the Ibira River Post is actually down to one vehicle after its reserve inspection expired, use that fact only if the post was considered for this assignment. The revised availability may require another source or removal of that route. Health and the towns keep the list and take the receipts.

**“I promised to put hunger first.”** *If pledged.* Dara shows which households can be reached through current payments and which still need intake. “Start with the names we can verify. I'll ask the towns for the rest.”

- **“Prepare joint civilian relief.”** Porper asks for a written municipal list and the latest state contribution.
- **“Cost military transport for the difficult routes.”** *If there are identified routes to assess.* Dara asks for estimates beside the civilian plan.
- **“Start with what the state can provide.”** *If a credible state offer exists.* Porper asks when the first coverage figures should come back.
- **“Bring me the costs before we choose.”** Dara keeps water, food, household aid and transport on separate lines. Add seed only if the agricultural office identifies a need.

### The joint relief proposal

*Dispatch on a complete estimate. If incomplete, retain the pending request and current authorized services.*

At the next meeting Porper turns the estimate so you can see the state and federal columns. Dara reads the towns covered. The mayor has sent a note asking who will answer calls from the places outside that list.

- **“Approve the joint civilian programme.”** *Matching civilian or open scope, fully funded and lawful.* Porper reads back the first route and the review date.
- **“Approve relief with transport on these routes.”** *Matching military or open scope with funded and lawful vehicles and crews.* The officer marks the handover points. Dara sends the verification instructions to the towns.
- **“Approve the state-funded first stage.”** *Matching state-first or open scope with state funds confirmed.* Porper names the places he can serve and gives the date for the federal review.
- **“Approve the smaller civilian plan.”** *Matching joint, logistics or open scope, with an affordable authorized subset.* Dara puts the uncovered towns on the next assessment.
- **“Revise the estimate.” / “Defer to the review.” / “Decline.”** Porper records the requested revision, review date or disposition. Dara confirms which existing deliveries the towns can continue.

### Back from the route

*On actual receipt or a reportable missed delivery, by relief scope. May run as a call.*

> **Clerk** — “The depot sent the load on [dispatch date]. The town signed for it on [receipt date]. I have household confirmations for the names on the second page.”

If only the town signed, she leaves that second page in the pending file. For a missed load she gives the recorded reason if there is one. A payment confirmation specifies the run, date and recipient scope. Ask **“Check the households still waiting”** or **“Send the dated coverage to Planning.”** Dara sends the appropriate request or verified summary. A failed delivery stays visible at the roads meeting.

## 4.2 · Before Six

*Vila Tamarin · vila-tamarin. Before a possible stoppage. If a strike is already underway, enter at the gate or the outcome that is known.*

Marco sets out cups while Dorival Sena moves a sack of flour away from the door. Sena has spoken with local representatives. He says a Sunday shift and the rates for subcontracted workers are still on the table, if the current demands include them.

> **Marco** — “Someone asked me yesterday if we could open in time for the first bus. She has to choose between waiting here and getting to her stop.”

> **Sena** — “I'll send you the local delegate's number. Ask her what her members have agreed she can put forward.”

Ask **“What would open earlier?”** Marco shows how many people the kitchen has on the first shift and what time supplies arrive. Ask **“What can the port decide?”** Sena points out the terms that require employers and workers to agree. Either question returns to the kitchen. Lara can stay to hand out cups before leaving.

- **“Cost food deliveries to the kitchens.”** Marco offers the opening hours and a list of sites that can take supplies.
- **“Cost help with housing and transport.”** Marco gives the clerk the bus stop and asks her to check capacity on the first runs. The office returns only the components it can administer.
- **“Invite the union and employers to talk.”** *If the parties can be approached on live demands.* Sena gives the delegate the invitation. She answers for the union under its own rules.
- **“Wait until we have a proposal we can fund.”** Marco asks the clerk to post the current hours.

### The gate at Katanaí

*If a live dispute warrants federal involvement. A settlement reached independently goes straight to the new rota. A funded and convened compact supplies a mediator if available. Ordinary talks and stoppage remain possible without it.*

The workers' delegate has the Sunday schedule open on a clipboard. She points out names that are on both weeks. The employers' representative opens the forecast for the next ships.

*Read D's effective officeholder on the date of this meeting.*

- **Marta Leal, independent appointment through the ordinary search.** Leal asks the operations clerk to compare the rota with the ships actually booked. “Give her a copy too. We can discuss an extra crew once we know what is booked.”
- **Irena Vale, if she is the effective director.** If her appointment fulfilled the recorded undertaking, Lara uses the resulting access and Mertens actually makes the introduction, he gives Vale the disputed terms before the meeting. Vale then brings the payroll officer. “Tell us which workers the new rate would cover. She's here to tell us what date payroll can use.” On ordinary contact Vale opens on the same payroll question.
- **Acting director.** The acting director reads the extent of the delegation from the file. “I can arrange this week's coverage. This other term needs the authority's signature.” Omit any offer beyond the delegation unless the competent authority participates.

> **Delegate** — “I'd like the hours and the pay on the same page. I'll take that to the members myself.”

- **“Offer a temporary schedule.”** *If management and the union can consider a specific lawful schedule.* Salgado or the available mediator reads the hours, rates and expiry to both sides and asks for confirmation. If they accept, post the start and expiry with the confirmed next meeting.
- **“Put a permanent settlement to both sides.”** *If the signatories, enforceable terms and confirmed port budget permit it.* The delegate takes the offer to the member decision. Management checks the first effective shift and the first covered pay period.
- **“Use the existing agreement.”** Salgado reads the existing terms. The delegate reports the members' decision when it is available. The director gives only confirmed changes to shipments.

### The new rota

*A signed agreement, independent resolution or the scheduled shift supplies the next report. If still open, use a new vote, proposal or service change for any redispatch.*

- **Permanent.** The delegate tapes the notice beside the entrance. “The hours start on [effective shift date]. Payroll says the first covered slip is [payroll date].” Use actual agreed dates. If payroll has already run, read only the rates and worker scope verified in the paid slips. **“Give both shifts the payroll dates.”** The delegate asks management for copies.
- **Temporary.** A worker asks if he is on next Sunday's rota. The delegate checks the end date. **“Keep the next meeting”** or **“Ask to bring it forward”** *if the parties and mediator are available.* The authority posts the agreed time. Expiry without renewal returns to the lawful terms and the workers' current decision.
- **Stoppage.** The director shows the delayed departures. If the union delegate is present she reads the provision members rejected. **“Ask for another meeting on that provision”** *if both sides and a mediator can attend* or **“Leave it with the parties for now.”** Where a separately costed temporary measure is available, offer **“Authorize it,” “Reduce the proposed scope,”** or **“Decline.”** Give the next port update from the responsible office.
- **Work continues under existing terms.** The clerk shows the current shifts. If the delegate requested another meeting, give its status. The workers can continue under these hours with demands still open.

### What the centre can offer

*Return only for the requested, complete local proposal. Use the existing municipal service notice if an ordinary hours update is available.*

In the municipal office Marco changes the kitchen hours on the board from the proposed time to this week's opening. The clerk has the provider list, start conditions and the districts the estimate leaves waiting. For transport she shows the covered fare or the service and route. For housing she shows desk staffing and eligibility.

**“Approve the funded scope”** sets the administering office and any confirmed start date. **“Cost an option for the places still waiting”** returns the uncovered scope for study. **“Keep the current service”** leaves the clerk with hours she can publish. End the visit here.

### The notice on the board

*Separate dispatch once per authorized measure and scope on the first confirmed operating milestone, or when the confirmed date has passed and the office verifies a missed start.*

The clerk puts the signed record beside the notice. Marco asks which places she can put on the board. Use one: the kitchens that received supplies on [receipt date], the route with support in effect from [service date], or the application desk that opened on [intake date]. Housing placements and payments require their own official confirmation. If the start was missed, the clerk marks the delay and gives only the reason the office established.

**“Publish what the office confirmed.” / “Check the places still waiting.”** On delay also offer **“Ask for the next confirmed date”** or **“Review the scope before setting another date.”** Return any materially revised scope to a fresh decision.

## 5.1 · Whose roads stay open

*Mognagna. Use a missed delivery, ongoing water access problem or documented dispute. Separate the current route from investment appraisal.*

The engineer takes the map off the municipal wall so you can see the depot sheet beneath it. A village is listed for [delivery date]. Its entry still has no receiving signature.

> **Engineer** — “I have a driver until Friday. After that we'll need another one for this road.”

If the resident at the depot was waiting on this route and attends, she asks whether to send her son back with the containers. The engineer checks the next confirmed stop before she answers.

If a Coronel's distribution network controls the road service, its organizer says he can put the village on the estate route. The engineer asks who would handle a complaint if a name is removed. If the municipality already runs an operating route, she opens its receipts and points to the villages served.

- **“Transfer the route to the municipality.”** *With funded staff, vehicles and authority.* The engineer calls the dispatcher for the first serviceable date and schedules publication. Keep any uncovered villages on the relief list.
- **“Use the existing network temporarily.”** *If the provider can legally serve it.* Ask **“Give the municipality the list and complaints”** if it can staff that work, or **“Let the organizer route it through the dry season.”** Set the actual review or end date. The organizer keeps operational control wherever the agreement gives him that power.
- **“Keep the current arrangement.”** The engineer tells the villages which dates she can confirm.
- **“Put the permanent water work first.”** *If eligible for appraisal or funded site work.* The engineer records which cisterns or storage sites to assess, or gives the authorized survey date. Review temporary deliveries against their own funds and capacity.

**“Check what the workshop needs.”** *If operating nearby.* At the workshop, the manager moves the next training class to the end of the shift, when attendants can still catch the bus. She points to the signed instructor agreement. If Lara requests a costed access or training plan, the office reports places, transport and operating costs before any award. Use actual attendance and jobs for the later report. Where service review identifies displacement or a land claim, send the eligible case to the voluntary relocation or land process. Require receiving capacity and household consent for voluntary moves, and lawful adjudication or voluntary registration for title.

### The coast in season

*Optional after a feasible access and services review.*

Above the bus office, an association organizer asks what time the return bus can run on market days. A guesthouse investor shows the proposed boat landing. The line to it crosses the footpath the association uses to reach the market.

> **Lara** — “Where would people enter on market day?”

The organizer moves the map toward the window and traces the public path with her finger.

**“Fund a locally run season”** if the municipality and association can operate it within the confirmed allocation. **“Negotiate the lease with public access and local hiring terms”** if the land rights, enforceable terms and consent can be secured. **“Cost a joint plan”** or **“Keep the current arrangements.”** If a funded festival actually ran and its organizer is interested, ask for available dates under a separately agreed event. The booking sheet returns only after operation and gives trips run, dates opened, hire starts and any access breaches.

## 5.2 · The queue in Tekoku

*Require verified internal migration, documented pressure on this school and a public residence proposal. No executive restriction.*

The registrar stamps a family's papers. A councillor holds up a draft asking for places to be set aside for long-term residents. The principal asks her to wait while the registrar finishes giving the family their receipt.

> **Principal** — “The class is full today. Here's when we can take another intake if the room and teacher come through.”

**“Fund the additional class under the current rules”** with an affordable staffing and room plan. **“Refer the documented access cases to Justice”** if named cases exist. **“Have counsel assess the residence proposal”** starts legal review. Any restriction needs a valid process and effective text following any required vote and ruling. **“Keep the present rules and budget.”** On the next intake give only the actual class opened and attending pupils, review disposition or rules legally in force. Keep names private.

### The room above the library

*Optional on an eligible Veldran heritage proposal.*

The curator passes a list of loaned objects to the library director. A festival organizer has brought a calendar and the hall estimate. They compare the dates when the library room is available.

**“Fund the festival edition.” / “Fund the museum and school visits.” / “Cost a combined plan.” / “Leave the proposals with the library.”** Confirm participants and event costs or funded staffing and loan terms before approving. After operation use the event expenses, visitor dates and actual school visits. At a separate Education check, Kaval can give the first date staff actually taught at a funded school. If the graduate from her briefing has started a verified placement, Kaval passes her the phone. “I started on [start date]. They gave me a bench by the window. I can show you the work next time you come.” A signed offer with a future date goes on Kaval’s schedule. Planning uses verified research capacity and confirmed partner terms for the Tekoku appraisal.

### The renewal meeting in Tapira

The producer puts his renewal letter beside the harvest figures. The rural workers' representative asks for the date workers will be paid. The producer checks the line the bank has marked for the next review.

> **Producer** — “If they approve the renewal, I can put Friday on the application.”

If C confirms a usable freight phase, the cooperative gives the actual opening date and says which producers can load there. If it remains pending, the cooperative uses its current shipping options.

**“Tie funded assistance to wages and continued production.”** The clerk asks for eligibility and paid-wage verification. **“Invest in water and equipment.”** Show the confirmed funding, scope and timing. **“Advance land redistribution.”** Show only a viable legal route, political backing and funded process. **“Keep the current programme.”** The clerk prints the current eligibility notice. At the next planting report, give paid wages and assistance, delivered equipment and training, or effective titles from their responsible offices. Use the actual harvest for trade and freight estimates.
