# Between the meetings

These brief encounters attach to the free-actions panel after the tutorial. Each requires an available office, an eligible subject and the resources shown before confirmation. PT, FC, IPI and EPI below are proposed cost categories. Prices and effect sizes remain provisional. Completing the same action on the same unchanged matter yields no additional benefit. Keep the ordinary calendar available when Lara passes.

## Spend presidential time

### Read the project papers

*With an active project and a new progress report.*

Marne has marked one milestone that still depends on a decision from the government.

> **Marne:** “We can send the contractor a date for our answer. The office is using the estimate from last month until we do.”

- **“Set a date for the outstanding decision.”** Marne asks the responsible office to confirm when it can bring the report. The next project scene uses the new appointment if it is accepted.
- **“Go through the estimate with me.”** He opens the revised figures. If the review identifies a real discrepancy, bring it to the relevant cost or inquiry decision. Nothing is delivered by reviewing it.

### Visit a regional office

*With an available visit and a documented local issue.*

The official has left a chair next to her desk. While you wait, she answers a caller who wants the address for an application.

> **Official:** “I've given that address out four times this morning. The notice in the old office still sends people there.”

- **“Have the offices use the same notice.”** She asks her colleague for the page that can be updated. The offices can act within their existing authority.
- **“Bring this to the next regional meeting.”** She sends the unanswered issue and the existing decision record. This can put an eligible question on that meeting's agenda.

### Review the answer to a petition

*Older petition with an office that can reply.*

The clerk finds the original request before handing you the reply. The second sheet bears a later address.

> **Clerk:** “We can send it now. Could you check which of those addresses the family gave us?”

- **“Send the verified decision.”** The clerk asks the registry to check the address. A reply records what the office actually decided.
- **“Ask the office to explain the part still open.”** The clerk marks the paragraph that awaits an answer. The outstanding question remains open.

### Call Celia

*Available when Lara promised to call and the agreed time has arrived, provided Celia can take the call. Show the time cost before confirmation. A promise does not count as fulfilled until the call connects. If Celia is in hospital, use the hospital contact route instead of this action.*

Amanda checks the day's appointments before she sends you through. Celia answers from home.

> **Celia:** "You made it. Your father said you might be busy until late."

1. **"How are you feeling today?"** `[flag: celia_call_status = 2]`
   ↳ Celia answers only from her current state. If no new medical result is available, do not add one.
2. **"Is Dad there?"** `[flag: celia_call_status = 2]`
   ↳ Your father takes the phone long enough to tell you what he has been doing. Celia asks for it back before he begins another story.
3. **"I wanted to hear your voice. I won't keep you."** `[flag: celia_call_status = 2]`
   ↳ Celia says she has dinner to finish and asks you to call when you have time.

Mark the promise completed only when the call connects. If the available time passes without a call, set `celia_call_status = 3`; the later family scene may acknowledge the missed call if Celia wishes to raise it.

## Use authorized public funds

### Maintain an operating service

*An operating site with a documented maintenance need and an eligible allocation.*

The manager shows you a work order that expires on Friday.

> **Manager:** “If we can renew it, we'll keep the present hours. Otherwise the next notice will have to give the reduced schedule.”

- **“Authorize the eligible maintenance.”** Confirm the expense and the period it covers. Retain service once the work can actually take place.
- **“Use the reduced schedule for now.”** The manager gives the office the hours it can staff. No funds are committed.

### Add capacity to a funded programme

*Available only where a programme can absorb the proposed funds during the term.*

The administrator gives you two versions of the intake notice.

> **Administrator:** “This is the group we can take today. The longer list depends on one more session and the staff to run it.”

- **“Fund the additional session.”** Confirm the cost and available staff. The office can issue places when it has a schedule.
- **“Keep the present intake.”** The administrator puts the smaller notice on the counter and keeps the remaining applications on file.

### Authorize a temporary response

*During an active disruption with a costed, lawful local measure.*

A district officer brings the estimates she has available for this week.

> **Officer:** “We can do this much before the regular service resumes. I need you to see which requests it will cover.”

- **“Approve the temporary measure.”** Confirm scope, cost and end date. Use the actual beneficiaries in the subsequent local report.
- **“Wait for the regular service.”** The officer issues the confirmed interim information. The disruption remains until its actual resolution.

## Use domestic political influence

### Sound out a caucus

*When a caucus is willing to meet and a named decision is pending.*

Mertens reads the title of the current bill before he puts it in his bag.

> **Mertens:** “I'll ask what they would need to see. I won't know how they'll vote until they have the text.”

- **“Ask what would bring them to the table.”** An actual reply can unlock a later negotiation. It grants no votes.
- **“Ask which clause they could support as written.”** The caucus may identify one or decline. Its answer applies to that clause.

### Put a small measure on the agenda

*With a prepared minor proposal and an official who can sponsor it.*

Livia brings the next list of business. A clerk has left a space at the end of it.

> **Livia:** “We can ask for that space. We still need to give them the version you want discussed.”

- **“Request a date for this text.”** The clerk schedules it only if the relevant office agrees. The act still needs the applicable approval.
- **“Keep it off the list.”** Livia returns the proposal to its folder.

### Ask the states to coordinate

*With an operational issue affecting at least two willing offices.*

A state representative puts down her copy of the service schedule.

> **Representative:** “We can match the hours. I need them to tell me which office will take the calls after five.”

- **“Agree who handles the enquiries.”** Use the division the offices accept. It can reduce an actual administrative delay.
- **“Bring the next proposal back together.”** An accepted follow-up can bring both officials to the next scene without committing money or powers.

## Use external political influence

### Ask for a preliminary trade meeting

*Where a government can receive the proposal.*

The foreign minister hands you the embassy's note. The host has asked what the delegation hopes to discuss.

> **Minister:** “We can name the proposal now. If they agree to hear it, we'll need someone from the department that can explain the terms.”

- **“Ask about the specific trade proposal.”** The embassy sends the requested subject. Only a positive reply opens talks.
- **“Keep it to the existing arrangements.”** Send an exchange on the current obligations without seeking a new deal.

### Support a cultural visit

*With invited participants, authority and any required funding.*

A curator waits while the embassy checks the list of works cleared for the visit.

> **Curator:** “I can send those on the first page. The owners of the others are still deciding.”

- **“Proceed with the confirmed programme.”** The host is sent the list it can actually announce. The visit can support the relevant relationship once it occurs.
- **“Wait for the remaining replies.”** The curator keeps the agreed works reserved only where the owners consent.

### Seek a partner's answer

*When an external agreement leaves a named issue open.*

The minister gives you the clause the other government agreed to review.

> **Minister:** “They were going to answer by the next meeting. We can ask them about this clause before we set the date.”

- **“Send a private request for their decision.”** Return with their actual answer. An unanswered request leaves terms unchanged.
- **“Keep the existing terms.”** The minister informs the office administering them that the schedule remains in force.

## Spend personal funds

*These actions use Lara's own available money. Keep receipts distinct from public expenditure, and apply affordability and outside consent before a transfer. Use established family or campaign circumstances when relevant.*

### Help with a specific expense

*A relative has asked for help with an identified expense or accepted Lara's earlier offer.*

Your relative puts the estimate on the table. One of the charges has been crossed out.

> **Relative:** “The second one was for something I can do myself. This is what I'd actually have to pay.”

- **“I'll cover the amount on this estimate.”** Show the amount from personal funds. If accepted, record the payment when made. The relative asks whether Lara wants the receipt or would rather they keep it.
- **“I can contribute part of it.”** Choose the affordable contribution. The relative reads the remaining sum and decides whether they can proceed.
- **“I can't pay for it.”** Your relative folds the estimate. “All right. I'll ask when they need an answer.”

### Contribute to a local cause

*Use a registered recipient with a stated need and an available way to receive a donation.*

A volunteer has brought the quotation for what the organization needs. She gives Lara the copy with the delivery charge included.

> **Volunteer:** “We've set aside what people brought in last week. This is the balance. I can send you the receipt after the money comes in.”

- **“Make a personal contribution.”** Confirm the amount and any required disclosure. Record a donation only on payment. The volunteer can place the order only once the organization has enough.
- **“Send me the organization's public appeal.”** She hands Lara a copy. This requests information without moving money or promising publicity.

### Pay an eligible campaign expense

*Use after candidacy where the rules allow a disclosed personal contribution and the campaign has an authorized expense.*

The campaign treasurer turns the receipt book around so you can see the entry she has prepared.

> **Treasurer:** “This is the hire charge for the hall. If you want to pay it yourself, it will be entered with the other campaign contributions.”

- **“Pay it from my own funds.”** Show the cost and disclosure rule before confirmation. The treasurer gives Lara the receipt once the payment clears. No opinion or endorsement follows automatically.
- **“Keep the campaign within its existing funds.”** The treasurer checks whether the hall can still be booked. If it cannot, use the venue and schedule the campaign can actually afford.

## The declaration

*One-time early meeting about Lara's own assets, with a later update only when a material change occurs. Salary, proceeds from a legitimate investment and any contested payment enter personal wealth only when received. The financial officer handles disclosure requirements. Public resources remain separate.*

The financial officer has brought the same declaration she sent you when you took office. She has attached a separate list for changes made during the term.

> **Officer:** “The salary entries are already there. I wanted to check whether anything else needs to go into the next declaration.”

*If a legitimate investment or sale is available:* She places the estimate beside the form. “If you want to proceed, check the restrictions on the second page. The amount on the front assumes the other party accepts the terms.”

- **“Keep my arrangements as they are.”** The officer returns the unsigned estimate. “I'll use what we have already received and the holdings you've already declared.”
- **“Proceed with the permitted investment or sale.”** Show the actual personal cost, restrictions, and disclosure terms. The officer asks for the completion record. Update wealth when the transaction completes, or record the actual loss if it does not pay out.

*If Lara is offered a benefit tied to a public decision:* A representative asks whether you can speak without the officer present. Where Lara lets the request be made, the representative states the benefit and the public decision they think Lara can influence.

- **“Decline the offer.”** The representative takes back the letter. If Lara asks for a record of the approach, refer what can be documented.
- **“Send the offer for review.”** The officer asks to see the original. “I'll ask the responsible office to confirm whether it can be accepted at all.” No personal money moves while eligibility is unresolved.
- **“Accept the proposed benefit.”** Show only where the offer is real and can be accepted in the story. State whether it is improper before confirmation. The other party can still fail to pay. Once the benefit is received, record its source and any outstanding request. Evidence that reaches an authorized inquiry can lead to a referral or hearing.

Later, a public disclosure, lawful order, or actual loss can change the funds Lara can use. The departure epilogue uses her accessible balance and ability to travel at that point.
