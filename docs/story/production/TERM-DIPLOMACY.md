# Terms and signatures

## The pages the bank will sign

*Turn 3.1 before the award of any contract that depends on external financing. This is the return and decision meeting for a complete project-specific proposal requested in the Pryce or Sadrah encounter. Show only a proposal with an identified lender, scope, total cost, repayment schedule, procurement terms, and any commercial or security rights. If no complete proposal arrived, omit the foreign offer and use the domestic or reserve decision. A consultation, expression of interest, or request for terms is not an offer. Proposed integration must retain the requested lender and project (`pryce_financing_project` or `zharan_financing_project`); only a returned proposal matching that request can be dispatched here. These request keys are provisional and are set only when Lara explicitly asks that lender for terms.*

Fontes has left the chairs opposite you empty while he checks the financing schedule with Marne. The minister has put the domestic estimate under a paperweight. Beside it is the schedule from the foreign delegation. The cover sheet names the selected project, the portion to be financed, the full cost, repayment dates, procurement rules and any rights requested in return. If a required term is missing or the offer has expired, do not treat the paper as ready for signature; ask for a corrected offer or use the domestic schedule.

> **Marne:** “The first work order is the same in both versions. The following orders depend on which money we can draw.”

> **Fontes:** “Then let me show the President what we have to sign before the bank releases it.”

The delegate comes in with an aide and takes the chair nearest the papers. Use the representative whose government actually made the offer.

*Zharani terms with a named supplier requirement.*

> **Sadrah:** “The bank has priced this on the supplier schedule we sent last week. If you want an open tender for those orders, I'll ask them to quote it that way.”

> **Lara:** “Could the first payment go ahead while they quote the rest?”

> **Sadrah:** “Only for the part already covered by the schedule. I'll ask them to put that amount on a separate page.”

*Federation financing with continuing conditions.*

> **Pryce:** “The first draw is available when the agreement takes effect. The later draws depend on the review. The conditions for that are the ones in this appendix.”

Fontes opens the appendix at the signed offer. “Treasury can meet some of those from our existing reports. The other ones require a decision from the government.”

- **“Accept these financing terms.”** Read the recorded obligations and the first eligible draw before confirmation. The delegate checks that the other signatory has the same schedule. “I'll request their signature on this copy. Your office will get the date it takes effect.” Confirm the agreement only once both sides have consented. Record disbursement when the payment is due and actually released.
- **“Bring back terms for the orders we can finance separately.”** The delegate writes down the orders Lara identified. Marne asks to see how the smaller offer changes the local timetable. Return with a costed counteroffer if the lender agrees to quote it.
- **“Use the domestic schedule.”** Fontes pulls the estimate out from under the paperweight. “This gets the first section started if the appropriation is in force. I'll send the contractors the funded dates after we've verified them.” Where even that section is unaffordable, show the affordable reduced scope or hold the award.

## Strawberry on the market schedule

*Conditional later negotiation. Show only when an actionable foreign technology or trade agreement asks for market access that would affect Strawberry. Name the actual partner and clause. Do not infer an offer from general diplomatic warmth, and do not assume the company will relocate or fail.*

The trade minister has set the foreign schedule beside Strawberry's current production plan. Works stands at the far end of the table with his own copy. He has marked the pages where local assembly and university research appear in the offer.

> **Minister:** “This is the clause they have authority to discuss. It gives their manufacturers access on these dates. The rest of the agreement does not require us to accept it.”

> **Works:** “If you sign that schedule, we need to know the same rules apply to us when we sell there. And I need time to decide which line can meet the new prices.”

> **Lara:** “Would the research partnership still work if we changed the market terms?”

Kaval checks the letter from the university. “The teaching agreement can stand on its own. The firm has not committed to the same contribution if its production plan changes.” She places the funding estimate beside it. Public research remains a separate appropriation; it cannot be counted as company investment.

- **“Accept the access schedule as offered.”** The minister sends this version for signature and asks whether the partner accepts reciprocal terms where they are included. Only a signed, effective agreement changes market access. If it takes effect, domestic producers face the listed competition and consumers may see the listed prices; no later company decision is assumed.
- **“Counter with a transition and reciprocal terms.”** Lara identifies the specific phase-in, review date, and reciprocal access she is authorized to request. The minister returns with the partner's answer. Existing agreements remain in force while the counter is pending; refusal leaves Lara free to accept the original offer or decline the access clause.
- **“Keep the technology agreement and remove this clause.”** The minister asks the partner to confirm the revised scope. If the partner accepts, research and training provisions may continue on their own funding and consent. If it refuses, bring back the whole offer for a decision; do not silently treat the remaining clauses as agreed.
- **“Decline the access offer.”** The minister withdraws only this proposed clause if the partner's offer allows it to be severed. Otherwise the linked proposal lapses and any existing treaty stays in force. The domestic trade rules continue; a separate temporary protection or trade investigation still needs its own authority and evidence.

Use the signed text and effective date in the public record. If market entry later changes Strawberry's production, staffing or prices, report the company's actual decision in the next company encounter. A signed access clause does not guarantee lower prices, a domestic contraction, relocation, or a new research contribution.

*Conditional return after the partner responds to Lara's counteroffer, request to remove the Strawberry clause, or submission of an agreed text. Works reads the effective-date line. “I can plan for a change when the trade office confirms it. Until then, the suppliers use the rules already in force.”

- **“Send the text both governments have accepted for the required approvals.”** Show only when both parties' wording matches and the offer remains valid. The ministers circulate the same version to each authority named in the agreement. Market access does not change until all required approvals, signatures and the effective date are confirmed.
- **“Counter the partner's named term.”** Show only while negotiation remains open. Lara identifies the exact term and authorized change. The minister returns with a further response; existing market rules remain in force in the meantime.
- **“Accept the original clause unchanged.”** Show only if the offer remains open and Lara has authority to accept it. Submit that text for its required approvals. It does not take effect on this decision alone.
- **“Proceed with the agreement that excludes market access.”** Show only if the partner accepted severance and the remaining terms have their own required approvals, funding and consent. Send only that text for approval; it creates no access right for foreign manufacturers.
- **“Decline the clause and any agreement linked to it.”** Lara's government makes no new commitment. If the partner's offer has expired or cannot be severed, the linked proposal lapses. Existing treaties and domestic rules remain in force.
