# Parties outside the cabinet

## A place on the agenda

*Optional early civic reform after the first budget. Offer when a municipal or legislative partner has raised civic participation or Lara asks staff to pursue her inaugural position. The programme needs a willing office, authority to establish the procedure, and funding if staff are required. Compare only enacted participation rights and operating procedures at midterm.*

Livia has invited a municipal clerk and two delegates who have been trying to bring a transport question before their council. The clerk puts a meeting agenda on the table. One of the delegates points to the last item.

> **Delegate:** “They said we could speak after the vote. We'd spent the morning here because we thought it was going to be before.”

> **Clerk:** “The rules give the chair discretion. The chair told me there wasn't room.”

Livia looks at the length of the agenda. “What do you want the office to do when it receives the next request?”

> **Delegate:** “Tell us whether we'll be heard and when. We can come back if we know the date.”

- **“Pilot a public submission procedure with willing councils.”** The clerk shows what her council can adopt under its present rules. Lara chooses a feasible pilot. Once the council confirms, publish the subjects eligible, available dates and where a written submission can be delivered. At the next meeting a clerk checks a person's name against the speaking list and explains when their item will be heard.
- **“Bring a statutory right to submit evidence before the vote.”** Livia asks which bodies the bill will cover. A sponsor brings the proposed text, including what happens when the allotted time runs out. Lara can submit a costed version, agree to a narrower scope after comparing it, or defer. Only passage and commencement create a new right. If it takes effect, the clerk gives a delegate the date of their hearing before the item is decided.
- **“Ask the councils to publish their existing procedures.”** The clerk leaves the current rules on the table. “I can show people how to apply. The chair will still decide how to arrange the hearing.” This improves access to information where carried out. It does not establish a right to be heard.
- **“Leave it with the councils.”** Livia gives the delegates back their papers. The clerk tells them when the current matter can next be discussed. No federal undertaking follows.

If a pilot or bill stalls, Livia gives the delegates the position in writing. “They have your submission. They haven't adopted the procedure we discussed. You'll need to use the current rules on this item.” A promise about citizens' voice remains separate from whether any procedure was authorized and used.

## The name on the envelopes

*Optional in Chapter 4 or 5 when the St. Veldra question has become a public municipal debate. A federal statement can influence it. The authority to change the name follows the applicable municipal and state procedures. Escalation in Tekoku or Mondrath requires its own conditions.*

The mayor has put two pieces of correspondence on the table. Both are addressed to the municipal offices. One uses the existing city name. The other has the proposed name typed above the same postal code.

> **Mayor:** “The second one came from the petition committee. They know we accept it. They'd like to know what you'll call the city if you come to speak here next month.”

A councillor who signed the petition has brought the committee's proposed wording. Across from her, a councillor who opposes the change has opened the minutes at the page giving the hearing date.

> **Petition councillor:** “We'd like the city's name to better reflect the people who live here. We can put the history in the public record before the vote.”

> **Opposing councillor:** “People have asked whether their documents will still be accepted. I'd like that answered at the same hearing.”

The mayor takes the minutes from him. “We can answer that before the hearing. The question on the agenda is the name.”

- **“Who has the authority to decide?”** The mayor puts the hearing notice on top. “The council hears the submissions. There are further steps on the back if the proposal passes.” Give the steps that actually apply. “We'd like your position. It won't replace that procedure.”
- **“What do you want people to hear before the vote?”** The petition councillor shows you the witnesses her group has invited. “One of them has offered to bring the records about how the name was chosen.” The opposing councillor looks down the list. “If they're bringing those, I'd also like to hear from the people who maintain the city archives.”

- **“I support changing the name through the city's procedure.”** The mayor asks if she can circulate Lara's statement with the agenda. If Lara authorizes publication, supporters can cite it and opponents can respond. The city's own vote still decides the proposal.
- **“I will use St. Veldra. I oppose the change.”** The petition councillor closes her copy of the wording. “I'll ask for your statement to be entered with the submissions. We still intend to bring the question to the hearing.”
- **“The city can decide. I won't take a position.”** The mayor asks the clerk to send Lara the hearing notice anyway. Both groups continue their campaign.

*If a lawful change passes and takes effect,* a clerk shows Lara the date from which the new name is used on municipal correspondence. The postal counter keeps a notice about the validity of existing addresses and documents. *If it fails or is held,* the clerk returns the proposed stationery to the office that ordered it. The hearing record remains public.

Where the debate draws a reaction from Tekoku, NF or Mondrath, use their actual public statements on the name and Lara's stated position. Do not infer separatism, diplomatic conflict, or approval changes from the council result alone.

## The room at the community centre

*Optional turn 4.2 meeting when local services, funding or an established inquiry gives Lara a reason to attend. The Tabernacle and its party continue to organize if Lara skips it. A public grant, a Communion service agreement, and a deal with the FFF are separate decisions. A referral requires independently documented grounds.*

The community centre lends its front room to the Tabernacle three afternoons a week. When you arrive, two volunteers are clearing the tables after lunch. One keeps a plate covered for a man who has gone to collect his daughter.

Pastor Kerner Muul is waiting near the office door. He asks a volunteer whether they can use the room for another twenty minutes. She checks a calendar pinned beside the door.

> **Volunteer:** “Until three. After that the children have it.”

Muul takes a chair beside the window. The volunteer sits at the other end of the table with last month's meal record.

> **Muul:** “We asked the municipality for a grant in January. They told us to apply again after the budget. Our deputies would like to know whether the next agreement will include centres like this.”

> **Lara:** “Who decides who can use the service?”

> **Volunteer:** “Anyone can eat here. Usually we run out around half past one. The names in this book are people we've agreed to deliver to.”

If an audit relevant to the centre exists, Renata joins for that discussion and brings only records she can lawfully share. If the Communion has submitted a service proposal, its representative waits for a separate appointment. Muul sets the meal record beside the meeting agenda.

> **Muul:** “We have the meal figures, the application and a proposal from our bench. Which do you want to discuss first?”

> **Representative:** “These are the places we can serve now. The other kitchens need equipment. The agreement says who can apply and how the municipality will check the deliveries.”

- **“Approve the eligible service agreement on these terms.”** Show only when the appropriation and required authority are available. The municipality issues the start notice for the approved sites; the other kitchens remain outside this agreement.
- **“Return the proposal for revised terms.”** The representative takes the marked copy. No service starts under the draft; the revised scope, funding and access rules must return for a separate decision.
- **“Leave meal delivery with the municipality.”** The representative closes the proposal folder. Existing public services continue under their current authority; no Communion delivery agreement is signed.

### centre-fff | The FFF proposal

*Show only if a party proposal exists and either no Communion service proposal was submitted or Lara has resolved that service proposal. Legislative support is confirmed only by the deputies after they see the actual bill.*

Muul puts the meal record aside and opens the FFF proposal. It names the grant and the clauses the party wants included in the next agreement.

> **Muul:** “If our members are to support the bill, they'll ask which part of this proposal you're prepared to accept.”

- **“Put the costed grant in the legislative agreement.”** Show the proposed amount, eligibility and reporting terms before Lara decides. Muul takes the text to his bench. He reports the members' actual decision after they have read the bill; this conversation does not count their votes.
- **“Bring back a programme any eligible provider can use.”** Muul asks whether the municipalities can administer it and which appropriation would pay for it. He returns with a revised, costed proposal. No grant or party support is recorded yet.
- **“Keep the grant and the bill separate.”** Muul takes his proposal back to the FFF. The party decides its legislative position on the bill independently; the centre may still apply under the public funding process.

→ centre-hub

If Lara has made no service decision, the volunteer puts the municipal application back in her folder. Muul takes the party proposal with him. Both remain open under their existing procedures; this visit creates no federal grant, service agreement or legislative support.

1. **“Thank you. I'll review the documents we discussed.”** Return to the office. Keep only the requests Lara actually made and any decision already authorized.
2. **“Let's finish here.”** The volunteers clear the table for the children's session. No further action is recorded.

## The chairs Trembolo brought

*Late Chapter 5 when the Capital Party's agribusiness wing has developed a real dispute with its urban leadership. Offer a merger or defection only if the departing group, receiving party and applicable rules permit it. The president can influence the terms through policy and negotiation. Members decide whether they leave.*

Vando Trembolo arrives with two members of his bench. He has asked for a fourth chair. An aide brings one in, but nobody sits in it.

> **Trembolo:** “One of our colleagues said she would come if the party leadership agreed to let her discuss the proposal. She called from the stairs. She's gone back to their office.”

He gives you a copy of the statements his group has sent to the leadership. The first concerns an agricultural measure. The second concerns who will speak for the bench at the next negotiation.

> **Lara:** “Are you asking me to decide the measure, or to speak to the party?”

> **Trembolo:** “The measure is yours to decide. We need to know what it says before we decide what we tell the party.”

If an FFF offer exists, one of the deputies shows Trembolo a note and he confirms what it contains. “They've offered to put our proposal before their conference. They want us to stand on their programme.” Use only an offer actually given. If none exists, the group may seek terms inside the CP or operate independently where permitted.

- **“I'll discuss the measure with the full bench.”** Trembolo asks for the text the leadership will see. If the party accommodates its wing, delegates may remain. If the fight has moved beyond the policy, they can still leave.
- **“Bring me a proposal your group can actually support.”** Identify the measure and the available concessions. Trembolo agrees to return with signatures or a tally. He can offer backing on that measure. A continuing coalition requires its own agreement.
- **“The government's position stands.”** Trembolo puts the papers back in order. “Then I'll tell them we have an answer. They can decide who they want speaking for them next week.”

*If the wing stays.* The missing deputy comes to the subsequent bench meeting. She asks whether the first item is still the agricultural proposal. The clerk reads the agenda the party actually approved.

*If members defect or join the FFF.* The legislative clerk brings the updated register. “These are the members who signed. The rest are still listed with the Capital Party.” Update seats, endorsements and public campaign options only from the recorded change.

*If they organize separately.* At the next vote, the clerk takes a request from their new representative. Mertens asks whether the new group has confirmed its position on the text. An old promise from the CP no longer binds members who formally left.

## The endorsement

*During the campaign, only if the FFF has an established negotiating relationship with Lara and can make a valid endorsement. Its members need not follow the party. If the agricultural wing joined it, use only the demands the enlarged party has approved.*

Muul asks the campaign staff for a smaller room. While they look for one, he reads the statement he has brought and makes a correction in the first paragraph.

> **Muul:** “Our conference authorized this wording. There's a copy for you. They'll ask me to read it exactly as it stands.”

> **Lara:** “Are there conditions in the statement?”

> **Muul:** “The policy commitments are on the last page. If we agree on those, we can announce the endorsement this afternoon.”

- **“Accept the endorsement on the written terms.”** Show the actual policy commitments and their consequences before confirmation. Muul asks which office can issue the joint statement. An endorsement affects the campaign only once announced.
- **“Ask them to endorse the published programme.”** He takes another copy of Lara's programme to submit to the party. If the conference declines, no joint statement is issued.
- **“Continue without their endorsement.”** Muul removes the corrected page from the copy he was about to give the press aide. “I'll tell our conference we didn't reach an agreement.” The party can endorse someone else under its own rules.

## Livia's conference

*Conditional late Chapter 5 scene. Use only after the OPP has scheduled a leadership ballot, Livia's work has given her a credible internal constituency, and she has independently decided to stand. The exact state gate remains provisional. Lara's personal relationship or endorsement cannot manufacture party support. The result follows the conference's vote. If elected, Livia has told the party she will leave the Chief of Staff role after the result is confirmed.*

The cabinet papers are stacked at the end of the table. Livia has put a card from the OPP conference beside them. It gives the date of the leadership ballot.

> **Livia:** “The conference has opened the contest for party leader. A group of state delegates asked me to stand. I've decided to put my name forward.”

She straightens the corner of the card, then looks up.

> **Livia:** “They'll make the decision. I wanted you to hear it from me before it appears in the papers.”

> **Livia:** “If they elect me, I'll leave the Chief of Staff role after the result. I won't try to hold both offices.”

- **“You have my public support.”** Lara may say, “I support Livia's candidacy for OPP leader. The delegates will make the decision.” The press office records the statement as Lara's political endorsement, not a government directive or a promise about appointments. The delegates remain free to vote for someone else.
- **“I'll keep out of the party's ballot.”** Livia closes the folder. “That's your decision. I'll make my case to the delegates.” The campaign proceeds without Lara's endorsement; their working relationship is not set by this response alone.
- **“What would you change as leader?”** Livia opens the folder she brought from the conference office.

  > **Lara:** “What would you change?”

  > **Livia:** “I want the party to publish its schedule and tell delegates when a proposal will be heard. I also want the leadership to check which commitments from the last election were actually kept.”

  Lara can then endorse her candidacy or keep out of the ballot. Remove this question from the menu after it is asked.
*If Livia is elected.* She brings the certified conference result and a signed notice to the president's office. “The delegates chose me. I'll leave the Chief of Staff role on Friday. I've put the decisions that need your signature on top.” Her departure takes effect on the date in the notice. Continue to [Until the appointment](TERM-GOVERNING.md#until-the-appointment) for the acting office arrangement.

*If another candidate wins.* Livia gives Lara the result before it is released publicly. “The delegates chose someone else. I'll finish this term as Chief of Staff. After that, I want a role the party chooses for me.” The result does not erase the work she has done or require Lara to offer her a new role.

*If the ballot is delayed or withdrawn.* Livia tells Lara which date or decision the party has confirmed. She remains Chief of Staff unless she submits a separate resignation. Do not treat a candidacy or endorsement as a completed leadership change.
