# The last address

*Turn 6.2, after campaign positions and coalition terms are settled and before polls close. A candidate may make a campaign address. A president who is leaving office may make a farewell through the same scene. Skip if the term ended early. The six topics use the current record and remain optional. Lara chooses whether to use the available passage or move on. Figures, named case outcomes and programme details come only from established public facts. The text may defend a decision. It does not establish Lara's private reasons. The economy matrix and the tier boundaries remain provisional until integrated.*

The hall staff leave one glass of water on the lectern. A campaign volunteer comes back for a copy of the schedule she left there. Through the curtain you can hear her asking whether the doors at the back are open.

For the farewell, replace the hall opening with the broadcast room. An aide sets a second copy of the remarks by the microphone and tells you when the broadcast will begin.

- **“I'll speak about the government's record.”**

  > “Thank you for joining me. Before this term ends, I want to tell you where the work stands and which decisions are still ahead of us.”

  `→ topics-hub`

- **“Keep it brief.”**

  > “Thank you for listening. There are still decisions due before the end of this term. I want to leave time for the people responsible for those decisions to do their work.”

  `→ close`

### economy | Work and the economy

When Lara raises this topic for the first time, begin with one opening. If she addressed the economy in her inaugural speech: “When I took office, I spoke here about the economy. I'd like to return to that now that we have the figures for the term.” Use “in my inaugural address” at a different venue. Otherwise: “During the campaign, people asked what the government could do about the cost of living and how it could help businesses hire. The figures I have today tell us some of what changed.”

| Direction | Growth | Stagnation | Contraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| State-led | “We put more of the investment programme under public direction. Output grew over this term. The next decisions are about which of those investments we can keep operating and extend.” | “We increased public investment. So far, national output has changed very little. I want the next decisions to follow the figures from the projects already in service.” | “We gave public investment a larger role. The economy is smaller than when we started. The projects we can still fund need to show what they contribute, and the budget needs to provide for the work already committed.” |
| Mixed | “Public works and private investment both had a place in our programme. National output rose. I want the next budget to show which parts of the programme can be sustained on the receipts we have.” | “We kept a place for both public and private investment. The national total has moved very little. Conditions in individual regions differ, and the next budget should address those differences directly.” | “Output fell during this term. The government has decisions to make about its spending, and there are businesses putting off decisions of their own. I would concentrate the available money on work that can actually go ahead.” |
| Market-led | “We gave private firms a larger role in the investment programme. National output grew. Our job now is to see that the public receives what it was promised under the agreements we signed.” | “We changed the conditions under which firms invest. National output has changed very little. The next review should show which firms have begun work and which commitments remain on paper.” | “We widened the role of private investment during a term in which national output fell. Some of the investment we expected has yet to come through. We need to make the next decisions on the basis of the financing available today.” |

Use a direction only if public policy actually changed in the way the passage describes. For a term without that evidence, use the confirmed output alone.

- **Growth.** “National output rose over the course of the term. The regional figures show where that growth took place and where there is still work to do.”
- **Stagnation.** “National output has changed very little since we took office. I want the regional figures to be available alongside the national total.”
- **Contraction.** “The economy is smaller than it was when we took office. The next budget will have to provide for existing commitments on the receipts now available.”
- **No comparable release.** “The statistics office has yet to release comparable figures for the whole term. The figures it has published are available for anyone who wants to examine them.”

→ topics-hub

### institutions | How the republic was governed

Use concrete claims about strengthened procedures only if at least one such change occurred. If there is no usable record for a passage, offer to skip the topic.

- **Institutions strengthened.** “We gave the public clearer ways to see decisions and challenge them. Some of those decisions went against this government. Those procedures should be available to the next government and to the people who disagree with it.”
- **Institutions held.** “Congress and the courts continued to do their work through this term. The decisions they published will remain available after this term ends.”
- **Institutions eroded.** “My government relied more heavily on executive authority as the term went on. Those decisions are part of the record. Where an order is still in force, the responsible office should tell the public when it comes up for review.”
- **Severe institutional crisis survived.** “There are decisions from this term still before institutions with the power to review them. My office will turn over the record it is required to provide. The hearings and decisions should be announced by the offices responsible for them.”

*Add only one appropriate sentence for a ratified representation settlement, and only where the speech includes this topic.*

- **Population weighting.** “The ratified text changes how population affects the distribution of Senate seats. The implementing schedule will tell each state when the new arrangements take effect.”

  *If Lara agreed to publish the allocation early:*
  - **“Include the publication commitment.”** “We agreed to publish the first allocation before the implementing bill comes before the chamber.”
  - **“Leave the commitment out of this address.”** No new date is promised.
- **Economic weighting.** “The ratified text changes how economic contribution affects the distribution of Senate seats. The published calculation should show every state how its allocation was reached.”
- **Decentralization.** “The ratified settlement transfers specified powers to the states. The implementing schedule will tell people which office is responsible as each transfer takes effect.”

→ topics-hub

### accountability | Public money and accountability

- **Strong safeguards.** “We put more of the spending record where the public could examine it. Keep the registers open. The people who use them will have questions about contracts long after this term ends.”
- **Mixed record.** “Where inquiries established a problem, the relevant offices have their findings. Some of the remedies remain outstanding. The public should be able to see whether the findings were acted on.”
- **Public scandal.** “There are public allegations against this government. The findings already made should be read in full. The offices still considering cases should be able to finish their work.” Omit the sentence about findings if none have been issued. Omit the sentence about continuing cases if none remain.
*If a public institutional-capture concern is established, make the response optional. Show the first option only when agreements or findings are already public:*

- **“Keep my remarks to the published record.”** Use the terms and findings already released, and name only the review still under way. Do not reveal protected material or imply that an allegation has been proved.
- **“I will answer for the decisions I authorized.”** “I made agreements to carry out this programme. The people examining them are entitled to ask what the government gave in return. I will answer for the decisions I authorized.” This does not disclose a secret deal or make an allegation public.

→ topics-hub

### neighbours | Kunasai and its neighbours

- **Regional bloc or multilateral agreement.** “We agreed to do work together with our neighbours. Where a matter requires each government's consent, the published agreement says so. Those governments have their own voters to answer to, as we do.”
- **Federation-leaning.** “Our agreements with the Federation are part of the work of this term. The next reviews should show what has been delivered and which obligations still fall to Kunasai.”
- **Zharan-leaning.** “We reached terms with Zharan on projects and trade. The offices administering those agreements should publish the construction and payment dates they have actually confirmed.”
- **Limited engagement.** “We chose a smaller set of commitments abroad. That gave us discretion over some decisions and left other work to be financed here. Both were costs we had to consider.”
- **Deep dependency during the first term.** “Some decisions now require the consent of our partners abroad. The agreements should make clear which ones. Anyone proposing to change those terms should explain which services and payments would have to be renegotiated.”

*Strait addendum, with confirmed unresolved tension and no terminal war.* “There are questions about the Strait still before both governments. For now, the port and passenger offices will issue their own notices on the services they can confirm.”

→ topics-hub

### services | Public services

- **Broad progress.** “More people can use the services our offices are reporting today. The new places have opening hours, staff and regular costs. Those are the figures I want kept in view when their future budgets are discussed.”
- **Limited progress.** “Some districts have seen changes. Others are waiting on the same decisions they were waiting on last year. For the work already approved, people should be able to get a date and the name of the office responsible.”
- **Deterioration or limited reach.** “There are offices where people now wait longer for a decision or an appointment. The departments have the figures. The next budget needs to show what it can restore and what it can keep open in the meantime.”

→ topics-hub

### safety | Safety and local authority

- **Major security gains.** “District offices have been able to resume work where the record confirms it. Their staff still have cases to handle. People should be able to find out locally where to take a complaint.”
- **Mixed or contained cases.** “Some of the cases opened during this term have reached a decision. Others remain with the investigators. The offices in those districts need staff who can keep the appointments they give the public.”
- **Persistent organized crime or loss of service.** “There are districts where people have to go farther to speak to a public official. We should give those communities current information about which offices are open while the cases proceed.”
- **Severe unresolved crisis.** “There are places where responsibility for ordinary services is disputed. People there need written information about their applications, travel and payments. The offices that still provide those services should issue it as soon as they can.”

→ topics-hub

### close | Close

*Candidate.* If Lara is registered and stated an actual programme position during the address, offer the first close. If she spoke only about the record or gave the brief opening, offer the second. A farewell cannot ask for votes.

1. **“Ask for my vote.”** `[show if: flag.registered_candidacy && flag.public_programme_position]`

   > “That is the programme I am putting before you. I ask for your vote.”

2. **“Thank them for listening.”**

   > “The election is your decision. I ask for your vote, and I thank you for hearing me this evening.”

   *For a farewell, omit the request for a vote:*

   > “Thank you for giving me your time this evening.”

Lara steps away from the microphone. The aide waits beside the lectern for the broadcast signal to end.

*At a supportive hall,* several people in the front rows stand. The volunteer at the back opens the second door so the audience can leave.

*At a divided or subdued hall,* some of the audience applaud. Others have started putting on their coats. The volunteer checks whether the aisle is clear before opening the doors.

*After a farewell broadcast,* the technician looks down at the schedule and tells you the station has gone back to its regular programme. The aide gathers both copies of the remarks.

*If an opponent responds before polling and the response concerns a topic Lara actually addressed,* the campaign aide brings the statement. “They've replied to the part about the programme. Would you like to send a correction before the morning papers?”

- **“Correct the specific factual error.”** Show only when a verified public fact contradicts the opponent's statement. The aide sends the correction with the cited record; the opponent's campaign may still answer.
- **“Restate the position I gave in the address.”** The aide sends Lara's actual words. No new policy or promise is added.
- **“Leave the statement unanswered.”** The aide files the response. The original record and opponent's statement remain public.

No fragment automatically fulfils a pledge, grants support or decides the election.
