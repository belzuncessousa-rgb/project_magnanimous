## opening

The cabinet room is on the same floor as your office. A member of staff opens the door and announces you, and the conversations around the table come to an end.

Your ministers rise as you enter.

> "Please, sit."

At each place there is a glass of water and a blue folder. The curtains have been drawn partway across the windows to keep the afternoon sun off the table.

Livia Harvan is seated to your left. As your Chief of Staff, she will coordinate the work of the ministries and make sure the decisions you take reach the people responsible for carrying them out. She is also one of the representatives of the Order and Progress Party in your government.

She opens her folder.

> **Livia Harvan:** "Good afternoon. I've asked each of you to give the President an overview of your ministry. We'll schedule separate meetings for the matters that need a decision."

She looks around the table.

> **Livia Harvan:** "If there's something particularly urgent, please let us know today. Otherwise, we'll try to keep this to two hours."

Mertens, seated on your right, quietly puts his watch on the table.

> **Caio Mertens:** "Is that two hours including coffee?"

> **Livia Harvan:** "Including coffee."

1. **"Before we begin, thank you all for being here."**
   ↳ > "I appreciate the work you've done to prepare for today. I look forward to working with you."
   ↳ A few ministers smile or nod in acknowledgment. Livia waits until the room has settled.
2. **"Let's hear where things stand."**
3. **"Two hours sounds reasonable."**
   ↳ Mertens puts his watch back on.

## economy

> **Livia Harvan:** "Alexander, would you begin with the economy?"

Alexander Fontes is the Minister of the Treasury. He has worked at the Central Bank and taught economics, and has never belonged to a political party. His reading glasses are resting low on his nose as he turns to the first page of his briefing.

> **Alexander Fontes:** "The economy has grown very little over the past two years. Businesses are reluctant to invest, and a great many people are looking for work. We do have reasonably stable prices at present."

He unfolds a sheet with several columns of figures.

> **Alexander Fontes:** "Our largest source of revenue is domestic economic activity, particularly the industrial and service sectors in Ibicuí. Agriculture and foreign trade make substantial contributions as well. Most of what we collect is already committed to the ordinary operation of the state."

> "And the debt?"

> **Alexander Fontes:** "That's the matter I'd like to discuss with you separately. Interest alone accounts for a significant share of our revenue, and the Treasury's figures still need some work after the handover."

Livia looks up.

> **Livia Harvan:** "How soon can we have a complete account?"

> **Alexander Fontes:** "Within the next few weeks. I can show the President what we have sooner."

She notes this in the margin of her folder.

> **Livia Harvan:** "Tobias?"

Tobias Marne, your Minister of Planning and Development, is a civil engineer and former mayor. His ministry oversees major public works and helps coordinate investment between the federal government and the states. He has brought several rolled maps with him.

> **Tobias Marne:** "We've had a number of projects under study for years. Some of them are ready to proceed, subject to the budget."

He moves one of the maps away from his water glass.

> **Tobias Marne:** "There are places where improving transport alone would make an enormous difference to the local economy. Then there are basic services. In the larger cities, housing has expanded well beyond the water and sewage networks. We have quite a bit of catching up to do."

> **Alexander Fontes:** "We should also establish the cost of completing projects that have already been started."

> **Tobias Marne:** "Yes. I've asked my people to look at those contracts."

## social

> **Livia Harvan:** "Owin, let's move on to health and social programmes."

Owin Dara, a physician who entered politics after years of working in public hospitals, represents your party. He takes a pair of glasses out of his jacket pocket.

> **Owin Dara:** "I'd like to begin with something I saw a few days ago. I visited a clinic outside Sugar Bay. The doctor there is responsible for six communities, and when she's away, patients have to travel to the city."

He puts his glasses on.

> **Owin Dara:** "There are places where we have adequate facilities. In much of Mognagna and Ibira, people have difficulty reaching even basic medical care. We also need to renew the funding for the existing family assistance programmes. A great many households depend on them."

> **Livia Harvan:** "Is there anything that needs to be addressed before the budget?"

> **Owin Dara:** "We're watching the dry season. It's started rather early. I'd like the President to visit Sugar Bay when there's an opportunity."

Livia turns to Helena Kaval.

The Minister of Education was a university rector in Tekoku. She has worked in both schools and higher education, and she is reading a report covered in handwritten notes.

> **Helena Kaval:** "We have two quite different problems. In some regions, a substantial number of children leave school without completing their education. In others, our schools and universities produce graduates whom we then lose to employers overseas."

She looks towards Fontes.

> **Helena Kaval:** "I realise both problems will have financial implications."

> **Alexander Fontes:** "Most things today seem to."

Kaval gives him a brief smile.

> **Helena Kaval:** "I'd be interested in discussing what we can accomplish within the first term. Building a new university takes time, but we can improve some existing institutions considerably sooner."

## security

> **Livia Harvan:** "Renata, would you take us through the situation at Justice?"

Renata Salgado sits opposite you. You worked together during the corruption investigation that brought you to national attention. She has since taken responsibility for a particularly wide ministry, covering federal policing, relations with the courts and states, and labour affairs.

She closes a file she has been reading.

> **Renata Salgado:** "There are investigations continuing from previous administrations, some involving public officials. I'd like to discuss with you how we make sure the institutions responsible for them have the resources to finish their work."

> **Renata Salgado:** "Organised crime is also a substantial problem, particularly in Ibira and in the larger cities. Our investigators have had difficulty working in areas where local authorities are under pressure from criminal groups."

Mertens is turning a pen between his fingers. He puts it down when she mentions public officials.

> **Renata Salgado:** "The third matter is labour. Several unions have asked to meet with us. I expect wages and conditions at the port to feature quite prominently."

She looks towards Livia.

> **Renata Salgado:** "I've sent you the requests."

> **Livia Harvan:** "I have them."

Aldric Voss, a retired general, is your Minister of Defense. He has brought a thin folder containing photographs of equipment and installations.

> **Aldric Voss:** "Our personnel are well trained, but a considerable amount of our equipment is approaching the end of its service life. Maintaining a presence along the longer borders is particularly difficult. I'll present our requirements with the budget."

He removes one photograph from the folder and passes it towards Livia.

> **Aldric Voss:** "My office also has responsibility for presidential security. If anyone in the President's staff has concerns, I would prefer they come to us promptly."

> **Caio Mertens:** "I'll let you know if Congress becomes particularly dangerous."

Voss gives him a polite smile.

The final member of this group is Darian Kesht, the Minister of Intelligence. He has spent much of his career in the service and retains connections with the National Front.

> **Darian Kesht:** "There are matters I'd prefer to discuss with you privately, Madam President. In general terms, foreign intelligence services are active here, and we have evidence of criminal groups cultivating contacts within public institutions."

Salgado turns towards him.

> **Renata Salgado:** "Anything that could support a criminal investigation should reach my office through the proper channels."

> **Darian Kesht:** "Where we can establish the reliability of the information, of course."

There is a short pause. Livia glances between them before making another note.
