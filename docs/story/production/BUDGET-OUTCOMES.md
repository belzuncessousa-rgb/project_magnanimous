# First budget outcomes

Working production design for the first budget. The bill can pass, be held for revision, or fail. Exact vote requirements, fiscal amounts, and interim spending authority need reconciliation with the legislative and financial systems.

## What carries forward

Use the proposal from the ministry budget meeting, Lara's earlier promise to Avros if any, her latest instructions on district allocations, and the coalition offer she actually accepted. Track support against the version a bloc has read. If a late change affects the basis of its support, give its negotiator a chance to confirm or withdraw it. Keep debt approval and procurement separate from passage.

| Lara's instruction | What can change in talks | What remains true after the vote |
|---|---|---|
| Publish allocations | The OPP can ask for equal access to submit proposals. Lara can agree, negotiate a smaller pool, or seek support elsewhere. If the pool disappears, a published list of proposals cannot fulfil a promise to restore funding. | On passage, publish approved allocations when they exist. An appropriation funds the programme. Each project still needs a decision. |
| Negotiate privately | Avros and Mertens can bring competing lists. Lara can authorize a single process for both benches, favour one bench, or reopen the terms. Favouring one may cost the other's votes. | On passage, use the approved ceiling. A privately discussed amount that appears neither in an authorized allocation nor a later lawful revision remains an outstanding commitment. |
| Keep national investment | Lara can offer ministers' explanations and a schedule for decisions on existing projects, seek other votes, or call the vote with the support she has. A schedule offered as a commitment can affect support and later trust. | A passed budget allows work under its national appropriations. The local school is eligible for ordinary review and may still be turned down. |
| Seek costs first | Treasury brings an affordable figure, a smaller figure, or a funding gap. Lara can resolve the choice in the coalition meeting or hold the bill. An earlier promise may still be due. | The final instructed route governs. If Lara holds the bill, no district appropriation follows. |

## Cross-checks for the vote scene

- A budget with a supported district programme can still lose if another disputed line costs the bill its majority. Show who raised that objection and when.
- A national-programme bill can pass when Lara earns enough actual support. Do not require a district concession in every run.
- An affordable reduction within the terms Lara agreed can proceed after the affected bloc confirms it. A cut below the agreed amount requires revisiting support and any promises.
- If Lara votes with uncertain support, show the known commitments and disputed votes before she chooses. A defeat leads to a revised proposal or a decision to proceed under existing authority for the time being. Deferral should offer a specific time or condition to return.
- On passage, Livia can give Lara a short account of what was authorized and what still needs action. On defeat or deferral, Fontes should identify one concrete delay tied to the actual bill. The first downstream callback can come from Avros when the school receives a decision, or from the project team when a funding decision changes its timetable.
