You find General Voss beside the steps to the courtyard. He has a folder under his arm and is watching the Jaguar Guard rehearse. You recognise the green armour from the inauguration. There are about forty guards on the gravel, and a few people have stopped at the fence to watch.

> **Voss:** “Good morning, Madam President. I had intended to show you the Treasury figures upstairs. Since the Guard was out, I thought we could start here.”

> **Lara:** “What's the rehearsal for?”

> **Voss:** “A presentation of honours. They'll do the entrance once more before they finish.”

- **“I'd like to watch for a moment.”**

  At the officer's call, two lines of guards turn to face each other. An officer walks between them towards the steps. They lift their halberds as he passes. At the far end, one of the guards lifts hers a little early, and the officer asks the last pair to do it again.

  A boy at the fence points to the guard.

  > **Boy:** “That's my sister.”

  The woman beside him has her phone raised. She lowers it a little so he can see the screen. You stay at the steps until the pair have tried it again.

  > **Voss:** “They'll do another rehearsal with the band. That usually takes longer.”

- **“Let's see the figures.”**

  Voss takes the estimate out of his folder. When the officer calls for the last pair to repeat the turn, he waits for the courtyard to quiet down before he speaks.

Voss sets the folder on the wide stone railing beside the steps. The first page has been marked by Treasury.

> **Voss:** “Fontes asked what we'd save if we stopped paying for the armour and the halberds. He also asked what we'd save if we had no Guard at all. We can answer the first question from this page.”

> **Lara:** “What else would he need for the second?”

> **Voss:** “A figure from the federal police. The work at the palace and the Residence would still have to be done, and they'd take over your protection as well. The Guard has three hundred and forty people. Most of them won't spend a day this month on a parade ground.”

He turns the estimate so you can read it.

> **Voss:** “I'd keep the Guard as it is. If you need a saving from Defense, I can work with an ordinary uniform for these occasions. I'd leave the protection staff and their training alone. If we abolish the unit, I'd be concerned about how long the police would need to take over the work safely.”

*Ask any of the following once, or decide now.*

- **“How much does the ceremony cost?”**

  Voss points out the replacements and repairs on the estimate.

  > **Voss:** “In a year, about what it costs us to put boots on a regiment.”

  > **Lara:** “Is that Fontes's comparison?”

  > **Voss:** “Mine. He asked me what else we could buy with the money and I told him.”

  He looks back at the guards, who are waiting while the officer speaks to the last pair.

  > **Voss:** “The armour goes back to the imperial Guard. We still use it when we present honours or receive a head of state. I'd be sorry to lose that part of the ceremony. I'd also be glad to stop putting off some of the smaller orders in my budget.”

- **“What do we know about the attempts?”**

  > **Voss:** “Four in sixty years, as far as the records go. Two under the military government and two since. The Guard kept the president alive each time. The last was eleven years ago.”

  He rests a hand on the folder.

  > **Voss:** “I can tell you what the Guard has done. I can't tell you how often you'll need them.”

- **“Why should the army still have this job?”**

  Voss looks up from the estimate.

  > **Voss:** “I understand why a president might want the police to have it. They're a civilian service, and their responsibility would be clear. My concern is the changeover. The Guard has people who've done this work for years. The police would have to build that experience.”

  *Optional follow-up.*

  **“Would you help them take it over?”**

  > **Voss:** “Of course. I'd ask to sit down with their director this week. I'd want you to hear from both of us before we settle the date.”

*Choose the Guard's future.*

1. **“Disband the Guard. Transfer protection to the federal police.”**

   Voss takes a moment to write down the decision.

   > **Voss:** “I'll arrange the transfer. The guards will return to regular army assignments. I'll put my objection in writing, and I'll speak to their commander myself.”

2. **“Keep the Guard. End the ceremonial armour and halberds.”**

   He turns back to the first page and marks two lines on the estimate.

   > **Voss:** “These orders can come out. I'll tell the commander which duties are staying before we send the figures back to Treasury. I'd like her to be able to answer her people when they ask.”

3. **“Keep the Guard as it is.”**

   Voss closes the folder.

   > **Voss:** “Thank you. I'll speak to Fontes. He'll still want a saving from my department, and I owe him an answer on where else we can find it.”

At the bottom of the steps, the officer dismisses the formation. Voss waits while two guards pass on their way into the palace.

> **Voss:** “There's something I'd like to discuss with you in private. Do you have another minute?”

You follow him into the covered arcade.

> **Voss:** “It's about the archives from the military period. The families are going to ask you to open them.”

*If Lara personally received Rosa Delmar's petition, she may say* **“Mrs. Delmar has already been to see me.”**

> **Voss:** “I heard. I'm glad she had the chance to speak to you herself. I've seen some of the replies her group has had from my ministry. They were very short.”

*If Lara referred the petition to Justice, she may say* **“Renata is handling the petition.”**

> **Voss:** “I'll make sure she has someone at Defense she can speak to directly.”

Voss takes off his cap.

> **Voss:** “Some of the officers I deal with today were already serving when civilian government returned. They understood that the records would stay closed under the settlement. I've told them I would defend that arrangement.”

> **Lara:** “What would you do if we opened them?”

> **Voss:** “I'd resign. And I would explain publicly why I'd done it. I thought you should hear that from me before you discuss it with the cabinet.”

- **“I understand your position.”**

  He nods and puts his cap back on.

- **“They'll stay closed this year.”**

  > **Voss:** “This year. All right. I'll be precise if I'm asked what you've said.”

- **“I'll examine the question before I decide.”**

  > **Voss:** “I'll send you the ministry's reading of the settlement. I expect Renata will want to give you hers.”

- **“My government will decide whether to open them.”**

  Voss looks directly at you.

  > **Voss:** “Yes, Madam President. I wanted you to know what my decision would be.”

His aide is waiting at the end of the arcade. Voss asks for the sheet on Ibira.

> **Voss:** “One item for the budget. Some of the boats our units use on the border are thirty years old. The crews are spending more time on repairs, and the smugglers have much newer boats.”

> **Lara:** “Is there a replacement request?”

> **Voss:** “There has been for nine years. I've asked for a new estimate for March.”

- **“Put the boats in the March request.”**

  Voss hands the sheet back to his aide.

  > **Voss:** “Include the repair costs this time, please. We only sent purchase prices last year.”

- **“Bring Justice and Kesht into the assessment.”**

  > **Voss:** “I'll ask them to join us. I'd like the officers who wrote the request there too, so they can explain what they're seeing on the river.”

- **“I want the wider picture in Ibira first.”**

  > **Voss:** “I'll send the district reports with the estimate. The border units can tell you what they're seeing. We'll need the local authorities as well.”

Voss walks with you as far as the door to the palace offices. His aide stays behind to gather the budget papers.
